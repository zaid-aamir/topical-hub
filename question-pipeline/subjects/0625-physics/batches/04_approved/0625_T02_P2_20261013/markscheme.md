## Q17

**(a)** [2]
- particles / atoms at the hot end vibrate more (1)
- these vibrations are passed on to neighbouring particles (through the lattice) along the rod (1)
- ALLOW: atoms gain kinetic energy and collide with / pass energy to neighbouring atoms

**(b)** [2]
- aluminium (is a metal so it) has free / delocalised electrons (1)
- the free electrons move (quickly) through the metal / travel long distances and transfer energy to particles further along the rod (1)
- ALLOW: aluminium conducts by both lattice vibrations and free electrons, glass only by lattice vibrations

**(c)** [2]
- the statement is incorrect: glass does conduct thermal energy (by lattice vibrations), but only slowly / much less well than metals (1)
- glass conducts better than good thermal insulators (such as air or expanded polystyrene) / glass is between good conductors and insulators (1)
- REJECT: glass is a perfect insulator

**Worked solution**
(a) Conduction in all solids happens through lattice vibrations. The atoms at the hot end gain energy and vibrate more strongly. Because the atoms are bonded to their neighbours, these vibrations are passed along from atom to atom, so energy gradually moves along the rod.
(b) Aluminium is a metal, so as well as lattice vibrations it has free (delocalised) electrons. These electrons gain energy at the hot end and move quickly through the metal, carrying energy far along the rod and passing it to atoms by collisions. This is much faster than passing vibrations from atom to atom, so aluminium is a much better conductor.
(c) Being a bad conductor is not the same as not conducting at all. Glass does conduct thermal energy, just slowly. In fact many solids such as glass conduct better than thermal insulators like air or polystyrene foam, but much less well than metals.

## Q18

**(a)** [2]
- particles in air (a gas) are far apart (1)
- so they collide (with each other) less often / energy is passed from particle to particle only slowly (1)

**(b)(i)** [1]
- particles in water are (much) closer together (than in air), so they collide more often / pass energy on more easily (1)

**(b)(ii)** [2]
- metals have free / delocalised electrons that transfer energy quickly; water has no free electrons (1)
- water particles are not held in a (regular, fixed) lattice, so energy is passed on only by (random) collisions between particles / not by lattice vibrations (1)
- ALLOW: metal atoms are closely packed in a lattice so vibrations pass on efficiently

**Worked solution**
(a) Conduction passes energy from particle to particle. In a gas such as air the particles are very far apart, so they collide with each other only rarely and energy is passed on very slowly. That is why trapped air is a good insulator.
(b)(i) In a liquid the particles are much closer together than in a gas, so they collide more often and energy can be passed on more easily than in air. Replacing the air with water makes the sleeping bag a better conductor, so the person loses energy faster.
(b)(ii) Metals conduct well because they have free electrons that carry energy quickly through the metal, and their atoms are held closely in a lattice so vibrations are passed on efficiently. Water has no free electrons and its particles are not held in a fixed lattice; energy can only be passed on by random collisions between moving particles, which is slow. So water is still a bad conductor compared with a metal.

## Q24

**(a)** [2]
- the temperature (of the plate) rises (1)
- because it receives / absorbs energy at a greater rate than it transfers energy away (60 W is greater than 15 W) (1)

**(b)** [2]
- net rate of energy gain = 60 − 15 = 45 W (1)
- energy = 45 × 120 = 5400 J (1)
- ALLOW: 5.4 × 10^3 J; 5.4 kJ
- ALLOW: 7200 − 1800 = 5400 J for 2 marks
- answer 7200 J (ignoring the energy transferred away) scores 0

**(c)** [2]
- Δθ = ΔE / (mc) = 5400 / (0.40 × 900) (1)
- = 15 °C (1)
- ECF from (b)

**(d)** [2]
- as the temperature of the plate rises, it transfers energy to the surroundings (emits infrared radiation) at a greater rate (1)
- the temperature stops rising when the rate of transfer away equals the rate at which it receives energy / reaches 60 W (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) The plate receives energy at 60 W but only transfers energy away at 15 W, so it is gaining energy overall. Its internal energy increases, so its temperature rises.
(b) The plate gains 60 J every second and loses 15 J every second, so the net gain is 60 − 15 = 45 J every second (45 W). Using P = ΔE / t, the energy gained is ΔE = P × t = 45 × 120 = 5400 J.
(c) Rearrange c = ΔE / (mΔθ) to give Δθ = ΔE / (mc) = 5400 / (0.40 × 900) = 5400 / 360 = 15 °C. Check: 0.40 × 900 × 15 = 5400 J, as required.
(d) A hotter object transfers energy away faster, for example it emits infrared radiation at a greater rate. So as the plate warms up, the rate of transfer away rises above 15 W and the net gain gets smaller. When the rate of transfer away has risen to 60 W, the plate loses energy as fast as it gains it, and its temperature stays constant.
