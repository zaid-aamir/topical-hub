## Q16

**(a)** [2]
- the rays are refracted / bent towards the principal axis (1)
- the rays converge / meet at one point (on the principal axis) (1)
- ALLOW: "the rays come together at the principal focus" for both marks
- REJECT: "the rays are reflected"

**(b)** [1]
- principal focus (1)
- ALLOW: focal point / focus

**(c)** [1]
- 15 cm (1)
- REJECT: answer without a unit

**(d)** [1]
- the rays spread out / diverge (as if coming from the principal focus on the side the light enters) (1)
- IGNORE: reference to the rays being bent away from the principal axis

**Worked solution**
A converging lens refracts each ray of a parallel beam towards the principal axis, so all the rays meet at one point. That point is the principal focus (focal point). The distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus is the focal length, so here the focal length is 15 cm. A diverging lens does the opposite: it spreads a parallel beam out, so the rays leaving it look as if they came from a point (its principal focus) on the same side as the incoming light.

## Q20

**(a)** [1]
- dispersion (1)
- REJECT: refraction / diffraction on their own

**(b)** [2]
- white light is a mixture of colours / frequencies / wavelengths (1)
- each colour is refracted by a different amount (by the glass) (1)
- ALLOW: each colour changes direction by a different angle

**(c)** [1]
- red (1)

**(d)** [2]
- all seven colours named: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (1)
- in the order red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (1)
- ALLOW: one mark only if the seven colours are given in the reverse order

**Worked solution**
White light is a mixture of all the colours of the visible spectrum. When it enters and leaves the glass prism it is refracted, but each colour is refracted by a slightly different amount, so the colours spread out into a spectrum. This is called dispersion. Red is refracted the least and violet the most. Red light has the lowest frequency and violet the highest, so in order of increasing frequency the colours are red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.

## Q29

**(a)** [2]
- increased rate of transmission of data (1)
- increased range (1)
- ALLOW: can be sent further for increased range
- ALLOW: the signal can be regenerated accurately / noise can be removed, for the increased range mark

**(b)** [3]
- noise changes the shape / values of the signal (as it travels) (1)
- a digital signal has only two values (high and low / 1 and 0), so the regenerator can tell which value each part should be and remove the noise (1)
- an analogue signal can take any value, so the noise cannot be told apart from the signal and is amplified / kept with it (1)

**(c)** [1]
- the signal can be regenerated many times (along the cable) without noise building up, so it is still accurate after a long distance (1)

**Worked solution**
Digital signalling allows a higher rate of data transmission and a greater range.
As any signal travels along the cable it gets weaker and noise is added. A digital signal only has two allowed values, so at each regeneration point the equipment just decides whether each part is high or low and sends out a clean new copy; the noise is removed. An analogue signal can have any value, so the equipment cannot tell which changes are the real signal and which are noise. When it is amplified, the noise is amplified too.
Because a digital signal can be cleaned up accurately again and again, noise never builds up, so the signal can be sent over a much greater distance and still be accurate.
