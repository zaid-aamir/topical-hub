**(b)** [2]

- the side of the Earth facing the Sun has day and the side facing away from the Sun has night; as the Earth rotates the city moves from one side to the other (1)
- REJECT: the Earth orbits the Sun once a day

**(c)** [2]
- the Earth orbits the Sun once in approximately 365 days (one year) (1)

- REJECT: summer happens because the Earth is closer to the Sun

**Worked solution**
(a) The Earth spins once on its axis in about 24 hours.
(b) At any moment, half of the Earth faces the Sun and has daylight, and the other half faces away and has night. As the Earth rotates, the city is carried from the sunlit side to the dark side and back again, so day and night repeat every 24 hours or so.
(c) The Earth goes once around the Sun in about 365 days. Because the Earth's axis is tilted, the city's half of the Earth leans towards the Sun for part of the orbit. The Sun is then higher in the sky and days are longer, so it is summer. Half a year later that half of the Earth leans away from the Sun and it is winter. One full orbit brings the same season back, so the pattern repeats every year. A common mistake is to say summer happens because the Earth is nearer the Sun. The tilt is what matters.

## Q19

**(a)** [2]

- Venus is small and Saturn is large (1)

**(b)** [4] any four from:
- the Solar System formed from an interstellar cloud of gas and dust containing many elements (1)
- gravity pulled the material in the cloud together / the cloud collapsed due to gravity (1)
- the cloud was rotating and flattened into an accretion disc (1)

- material in the disc clumped together due to gravity to form the planets (1)

- ALLOW: gases were blown away from the region near the Sun

**Worked solution**
(a) Venus is small and rocky. Saturn is large and made mostly of gas. All four planets nearest the Sun are small and rocky, and all four furthest from the Sun are large and gaseous.
(b) The Solar System began as a huge interstellar cloud of gas and dust containing many different elements. Gravity pulled this material together and the cloud collapsed. To get full marks, mention gravity, the rotating cloud and the accretion disc, and then link them to the rocky inner planets and gaseous outer planets.

## Q22

**(a)** [1]

**(b)** [3]
- as the comet moves from Q to P it gets closer to the Sun, so its gravitational potential energy decreases (1)
- the total energy of the comet is conserved / stays the same (1)
- so its kinetic energy increases and the comet speeds up (1)
- ALLOW: gravitational potential energy is transferred to kinetic energy for the second and third marks if both ideas are clearly present

**(c)** [1]
- its distance from the Sun stays (almost) the same, so its gravitational potential energy (and so its kinetic energy) stays (almost) constant (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) An object in an elliptical orbit moves fastest when it is closest to the Sun, which is at P.
(b) As the comet falls inwards from Q towards P, it gets closer to the Sun, so its gravitational potential energy decreases.
