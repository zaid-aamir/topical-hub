## Q1

**Rationale**
In a series circuit the current is the same at every point, so the second ammeter also reads 0.30 A. A (0.10 A) and C (0.15 A) come from the idea that the lamps "use up" current, which is wrong: lamps transfer energy, not charge. D (0.90 A) wrongly adds a share for each lamp.

## Q2

## Q11

## Q13

**Rationale**

## Q29

**(a)** [2]
- a north pole (1)
- the induced e.m.f. / current opposes the change causing it, so end P repels the approaching north pole (1)

**(b)** [1]
- a south pole (1)

**(c)** [2]
- there is a (repulsive) force opposing the motion of the magnet, so work must be done against it (1)
- this work is transferred to electrical energy in the circuit (and then to thermal energy in the resistor); energy is conserved (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) The direction of an induced e.m.f. always opposes the change causing it. The change is a north pole coming closer, so the induced current makes end P a north pole, which pushes back on the approaching magnet.
(b) Now the change is the north pole moving away. To oppose this, end P becomes a south pole, which attracts the magnet and tries to hold it back.
(c) Because the coil always pushes against the motion, the person must exert a force over a distance, which is work done. Energy cannot be created: this work is the source of the electrical energy carried by the induced current, which is then transferred to the surroundings as thermal energy in the resistor. If there were no opposing force, electrical energy would appear from nothing.
