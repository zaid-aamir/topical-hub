## Q16

**Worked solution**
Solid to liquid is melting. Liquid to gas is boiling (or evaporation, if it happens only at the surface below the boiling point). Liquid to solid is solidification, also called freezing. ## Q19

**(a)** [2]
- in steel, strong forces (of attraction) hold the particles in fixed positions so they can only vibrate (1)
- in water, the forces are weaker / particles are not held in fixed positions, so the particles can move past / slide over each other (1)

**(b)** [2]
- particles in air (a gas) are far apart, with large spaces between them, so they can be pushed closer together (1)
- particles in water (a liquid) are close together / touching, with very little space between them (1)

**(c)** [2]
- particles collide with the walls and each collision exerts a force on the wall (1)
- pressure is the total force (from all the collisions) per unit area (of wall) (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) The properties of a substance depend on the forces between its particles and how the particles move. In a solid such as steel, strong attractive forces hold each particle in a fixed position, so the bar keeps its shape. In a liquid the particles are still close together but they are not held in fixed positions, so they slide past each other and the liquid flows into the shape of its container.
(b) In a gas the particles are far apart, so there is plenty of empty space and the gas can be squashed. In a liquid the particles are already almost touching, so the liquid can hardly be compressed.
(c) Air particles move randomly and keep hitting the walls of the syringe. Each collision pushes on the wall with a small force. The very large number of collisions gives a total force on the wall, and the force acting on each unit area of wall is the pressure.

**Worked solution**
