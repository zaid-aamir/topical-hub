## Q11
- answer: D

**Rationale**

## Q12
- answer: C

**Rationale**
The α-particle removes 2 protons and 2 neutrons, so the proton number falls by 2 and the nucleon number by 4. Each β-decay turns a neutron into a proton, raising the proton number by 1 and leaving the nucleon number unchanged. Overall the proton number is unchanged and the nucleon number is 4 less. A ignores the β-decays, B treats β-decay as lowering the proton number, and D counts each β-decay as +2.

**Worked solution**
α-decay: proton number − 2, nucleon number − 4. First β-decay: proton number + 1, nucleon number + 0. Second β-decay: proton number + 1, nucleon number + 0. Total: proton number − 2 + 1 + 1 = 0 (the same); nucleon number − 4. The final nucleus is an isotope of the original element.

## Q16

**(a)** [2]
- (small) positively charged nucleus (at the centre) (1)
- negatively charged electrons orbit (around) the nucleus (1)

**(b)** [1]
- 11 (1)

**(c)** [2]
- positive ion (1)
- it now has more protons than electrons / more positive charges than negative charges (1)
- ALLOW: it has 11 protons but only 10 electrons
- REJECT: it gains a proton / loses a negative proton

**Worked solution**
(a) An atom has a tiny nucleus at its centre, which is positively charged. Negatively charged electrons move in orbits around the nucleus.
(b) A neutral atom has no overall charge, so the number of electrons (each –1) must equal the number of protons (each +1). So there are 11 electrons.
(c) Losing an electron removes one negative charge. The atom now has 11 protons (+11) and 10 electrons (–10), so its overall charge is +1. Atoms that lose electrons form positive ions.

## Q18

**(a)** [1]

**(b)** [1]
- 38 (1)

**(c)** [2]
- total nucleon number before = 1 + 235 = 236 / 140 + 94 + x = 236 (1)

**(d)** [2]
- the total mass after the reaction is less than the total mass before (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) A heavy uranium nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei after absorbing a neutron. Splitting a nucleus is nuclear fission.
(b) Proton numbers must balance: 0 + 92 = 54 + Z + 0, so Z = 92 − 54 = 38.
(c) Nucleon numbers must balance. Before: 1 + 235 = 236. After: 140 + 94 + x(1) = 234 + x. So 234 + x = 236 and x = 2. Two neutrons are released. Check the proton numbers with x = 2: 54 + 38 + 2 × 0 = 92, which matches.
(d) In fission the total mass of the products is slightly less than the total mass of the particles before the reaction. This mass is not lost for nothing: energy is released, mostly as kinetic energy of the products.

**Worked solution**

## Q23

**(a)** [3]
- no α-particles: the paper makes (almost) no difference to the count rate (1)
- β-particles: the 3 mm of aluminium greatly reduces the count rate (1)
- γ-radiation: the count rate does not fall to zero with aluminium / the count rate hardly changes when the aluminium is doubled to 6 mm (1)

**(b)** [1]

**Worked solution**
(a) α-particles are stopped by a sheet of paper. ## Q24

**(a)** [2]
- α-particles have a large (relative) charge of +2, so they exert large (electric) forces on electrons in atoms / pull electrons out of atoms (1)
- α-particles have a large kinetic energy (because of their large mass), so they can ionise many atoms (giving up some energy each time) (1)
- ALLOW: α-particles are massive and slow, so they spend longer near each atom

**(b)(i)** [2]
- downwards / towards the bottom of the page (1)
- α-particles are positively charged, the opposite charge to β-particles (1)

**(b)(ii)** [2]
- the deflection of the α-particles is (much) smaller (1)
- α-particles have a much greater mass (than β-particles) (1)
- ALLOW: α-particles have much greater momentum

**(c)** [1]
- not deflected because γ-radiation has no charge (1)
- REJECT: not deflected because it is too fast

**Worked solution**
(a) Ionisation happens when radiation removes electrons from atoms. An α-particle has a charge of +2, the largest of the three types, so it attracts electrons in the atoms it passes very strongly. It also has a large kinetic energy because it has a large mass. Each ionisation uses some of this energy, so an α-particle ionises a large number of atoms in a very short distance before it stops.
(b)(i) A magnetic field pushes a moving positive charge in the opposite direction to a moving negative charge travelling the same way. β-particles are negative and go upwards, so α-particles, which are positive, are deflected downwards.
(b)(ii) An α-particle has thousands of times the mass of a β-particle, so it is much harder to change its direction. Its deflection is much smaller.
(c) γ-radiation is an uncharged electromagnetic wave, so the magnetic field exerts no force on it and it continues in a straight line.

- ALLOW: nuclear radiation is emitted, for the second mark

**(b)** [2]

- ALLOW: the proton number changes (by 2)

**(c)** [1]

**Worked solution**

**(a)** [1]

**(b)** [3]

## Q30

**(a)** [2]

- ALLOW: radiation burns / radiation sickness
- REJECT: makes the person radioactive

**(b)** [2]

**(c)** [2]

**Worked solution**

These steps keep the source further away from people and reduce the time anyone is exposed.
