**(a)** [3]

- the prongs make the air particles next to them vibrate (1)

## Q27

**(a)** [2]
- (i) a region where the air particles are closer together than normal / a region of higher pressure (1)
- (ii) a region where the air particles are further apart than normal / a region of lower pressure (1)

**(b)** [2]
- as the cone moves forwards it pushes the air particles together, producing a compression (1)
- as the cone moves backwards the air particles spread out, producing a rarefaction (1)

**(c)** [2]
- f = v / λ = 340 / 0.68 (1)
- 500 Hz (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Sound is a longitudinal wave. In a compression the particles are squashed closer together than normal, so the pressure is higher. In a rarefaction the particles are spread further apart than normal, so the pressure is lower.
(b) When the cone moves forwards, it pushes the air in front of it, squeezing the particles together into a compression. When it moves back, it leaves more space, so the particles spread out into a rarefaction. Each vibration of the cone sends out one compression and one rarefaction.
(c) The distance between the centres of neighbouring compressions is one wavelength, so λ = 0.68 m. From v = fλ, f = v / λ = 340 m / s / 0.68 m = 500 Hz. Check: 500 Hz × 0.68 m = 340 m / s.
