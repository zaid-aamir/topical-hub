Slip rings turn with the coil and the fixed brushes slide on them, so the coil stays connected to the external circuit while it rotates and the wires never twist. ## Q15
- answer: B

**Rationale**
For the same power, increasing the voltage 5 times makes the current 5 times smaller. The power lost is I^2R, so it falls by 5^2 = 25 times. A forgets that the loss depends on the current squared. C and D come from wrongly using the transmission voltage in place of the voltage across the cables, which makes it look as if the loss increases.

**Worked solution**
Power transmitted = IV, so with the same power, I is inversely proportional to V. Voltage × 5 means current ÷ 5.
Power lost in the cables = I^2R. With R unchanged, I ÷ 5 means I^2 ÷ 25, so the power lost becomes 25 times smaller.

## Q24

**(a)** [2]

**(b)** [3]

- every half turn / when the plane of the coil is vertical / at right angles to the field (1)
- so the forces on each side of the coil keep the turning effect in the same rotational direction (and the coil keeps turning the same way) (1)
- ALLOW: without the commutator, the coil would rock back and forth / stop at the vertical position

- to make a (sliding) electrical contact between the fixed supply wires and the rotating commutator (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Current flows along one long side of the coil and back along the other, so the currents in the two sides are in opposite directions. (c) The brushes are fixed. They press on the rotating commutator so that current can pass between the supply and the moving coil.
