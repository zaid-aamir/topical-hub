## Q24
**(a)** The current flows in opposite directions in the two long sides of the coil (those at right angles to the field). Each side experiences a force at right angles to the field and the current, and these forces are in opposite directions (one up, one down), so they produce a turning effect (moment) that starts the coil rotating about the axle.
**(b)** Without the commutator, when the coil passes the vertical position the forces on each side would reverse their effect relative to the coil (the side that moved up would now be pushed back down), so the coil would stop or oscillate. The split-ring commutator reverses the direction of the current in the coil every half turn, as the coil passes the vertical position. This keeps the force on the side on one side of the axle always upwards and the other always downwards, so the turning effect stays in the same direction and the coil continues rotating the same way.
**(c)** The brushes make sliding electrical contact between the power supply and the rotating split-ring commutator (and so the coil), so current can enter and leave the coil.

- confidence: high
