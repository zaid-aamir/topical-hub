# 0625 Physics — subject rules (DRAFT, from the syllabus only)

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for exams in 2026, 2027 and 2028, Version 2 (published December 2025). Page numbers refer to that PDF. Learning points are cited by `point_id` from `config/syllabus_map.json`. Status: draft for owner checking against the PDF.

Tiers: Core content is tested on Papers 1 and 3; Core + Supplement (Extended) on Papers 2 and 4 (pp. 9, 42). A Core-tier question must use Core points only.

## 1. Exam data given

- **None stated.** The syllabus does not describe any formula sheet, equation list, data booklet or table of constants supplied in the exam (checked pp. 9–11, 12–41, 42–55). It does not state that a value of g is given; 1.2.8 requires candidates to know g ≈ 9.8 m / s^2.
- Calculators may be used in all parts of the examination (p. 52).
- Data that questions themselves supply: the content expects candidates to work from "given data" at 1.2.5, 1.2.10 (given data or graph shape), 1.4.3, 1.4.4 (density data), 5.2.4.1 (tables or decay curves), 5.2.4.2 (data or decay curves) and 6.1.2.8 (planetary data: orbital distance, orbital duration, density, surface temperature, gravitational field strength at the surface).
- Where data is supplied, candidates use units consistent with the units supplied and do not convert to other systems unless asked (p. 54).
- Generator rule: any numerical value a question needs that is not in the "must be recalled" list below (e.g. a specific heat capacity, a density, a refractive index, the value of g to use in a calculation) must be stated in the question.

Values and facts stated in the content (candidates know them):
- g ≈ 9.8 m / s^2 near the Earth's surface (1.2.8, Core).
- Absolute zero −273 °C (2.1.2.2); melting and boiling temperatures of water at standard atmospheric pressure (2.2.3.2).
- Speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum 3.0 × 10^8 m / s, approximately the same in air (3.3.6, Supplement). Core candidates only know all EM waves travel at the same high speed in a vacuum (3.3.2).
- Audible range 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz (3.4.3); speed of sound in air approximately 330–350 m / s (3.4.5); ultrasound above 20 kHz (3.4.9).
- Relative charges of proton, neutron, electron: +1, 0, –1 (5.1.2.2).
- Earth rotates once in approximately 24 hours, orbits the Sun in approximately 365 days; Moon orbits the Earth in approximately one month (6.1.1.1–6.1.1.3).
- One light-year = 9.5 × 10^15 m (6.2.2.2, Supplement); Milky Way diameter approximately 100 000 light-years (6.2.3.1); H0 ≈ 2.2 × 10^-18 per second (6.2.3.10, Supplement).
- Mathematics to recall (p. 52): circumference of a circle; areas of a rectangle, triangle and circle; volumes of a rectangular block and a cylinder; Pythagoras' theorem; sine, cosine, tangent and inverses (Extended only).

Explicitly NOT required: F = mv^2 / r (1.5.1.12); an understanding of the elastic limit (1.5.1.10); β+ (5.2.2.2); end effects of parallel-plate fields (4.2.1.10c); gas to solid and solid to gas changes of state (2.1.1.2); background radiation in Core half-life calculations (5.2.4.1); values of mass and energy changes in fission/fusion (5.1.2.6).

## 3. Notation and units

- Use the symbols and units in the syllabus lists (pp. 50–51). Core: length l, h, d, s, x (km, m, cm, mm); area A (m^2, cm^2); volume V (m^3, cm^3, dm^3); weight W (N); mass m, M (kg, g); time t (h, min, s); density ρ (g / cm^3, kg / m^3); speed u, v (km / h, m / s, cm / s); acceleration a (m / s^2); acceleration of free fall g (m / s^2); force F (N); gravitational field strength g (N / kg); moment of a force (Nm); work done W (J, kJ, MJ); energy E (J, kJ, MJ, kW h); power P (W, kW, MW); pressure p (N / m^2, N / cm^2); temperature θ, T (°C, K); frequency f (Hz, kHz); wavelength λ (m, cm); focal length f (m, cm); angles i, r, c (degree (°)); potential difference/voltage V (V, mV, kV); current I (A, mA); e.m.f. E (V); resistance R (Ω); count rate (counts / s, counts / min); half-life (s, min, h, days, weeks, years).
- Supplement only: mass in mg; time in ms, μs; spring constant k (N / m, N / cm); momentum p (kg m / s); impulse (Ns); pressure in Pa; specific heat capacity c (J / (g °C), J / (kg °C)); wavelength in nm; refractive index n; charge Q (C); Hubble constant H0 (s^-1). Do not use these in Core-tier questions.
- Prefixes: all candidates M, k, c, m; Extended candidates also G, µ, n (p. 50). Core questions must not use G, µ or n.
- Plain-text writing: powers with `^` (m / s^2, 3.0 × 10^8 m / s, I^2R, s^-1); `×` for multiplication; Unicode Greek (ρ, λ, θ, Ω, Δ, α, β, γ, π, µ); subscripts inline (Ek, ΔEp, Vp, Ns, H0, R1); fractions in one line with ` / ` and brackets where needed, e.g. c = ΔE / (mΔθ); nuclide notation as ^A_Z X (e.g. ^14_6 C).
- Units written as in the syllabus, with a space between number and unit and the syllabus's solidus style: `m / s`, `kg / m^3`, `N / kg`, `J / (kg °C)`, `kW h`.
- Use dm^3, never litre or l (p. 54). SI or BIPM-approved units only; no inches or °F (p. 54).
- Decimal marker is a dot on the line. Numbers 1000–9999 have no comma or space; 10 000 and above use a space between groups of three digits, never commas (e.g. 4 256 789) (p. 54).
- Table headings and graph axes: quantity / unit, e.g. `time / s`; no units in the body of a table (p. 53). Ratios as x : y (p. 53).
- Use Δ for changes in a variable (p. 52).
- Charges on particles: β-particles mean β– (electrons); proton +1, neutron 0, electron –1.

## 4. Calculation conventions

- Numerical answers are written as decimals (or percentages if appropriate), not fractions (p. 52).
- Only the final answer is rounded (p. 52). Use standard form where appropriate (p. 52).
- Significant figures of a calculated answer = the least number of significant figures in the raw data used in that calculation (p. 53). Gradients to two or three significant figures (p. 54).
- Every measured or calculated quantity carries a correct unit (p. 53).
- Units in answers must be consistent with the units supplied in the question; no conversion to other systems unless the question requires it (p. 54).
- Results agree "within the limits of experimental accuracy" if within ± 10% (p. 44).
- Readings from scales and graphs to half the smallest division / half the smallest grid square (pp. 53–54).
- Extended only: trigonometric functions; gradient of a curve by drawing a tangent (pp. 52–53).

## 5. Diagrams

- `essential` points are out of scope — do not set questions on them: 1.2.4, 1.5.1.2, 3.2.3.3, 3.2.3.6, 4.1.6, 4.2.4.4, 4.3.1.1, 4.3.1.2, 4.5.2.2 (9 of 324 points, 2.8%). See `config/diagram_coverage.md`.
- `partial` points (80): describe the situation fully in words (positions, directions such as "into the page", distances, angles) or give a Markdown data table instead of a graph (e.g. time / s against speed / m / s). Never write "see the diagram". Circuits must be described component by component (e.g. "a 6.0 V cell in series with a 4.0 Ω resistor and two 6.0 Ω resistors connected in parallel with each other").
- Graph-based points (1.2.5–1.2.7, 1.2.10, 1.2.11, 1.5.1.10, 2.1.3.3, 5.2.4.1, 5.2.4.2): give the data as a table; areas/gradients are worked from tabulated values.

## 6. Subject-specific checks for the verifier

- Every equation used in a question or answer is either in the "must be recalled" list (Section 2) or stated in the question. Equations not on the syllabus (e.g. F = mv^2 / r, v^2 = u^2 + 2as, s = ut + ½at^2, Q = mL) must be given in full in the question if used at all — prefer not to use them.
- Tier check: a Core question uses only Core points, Core equations, Core units and Core prefixes. Supplement-only equations (e.g. F = ma, p = mv, Ek = ½mv^2, ΔEp = mgΔh, I = Q / t, c = ΔE / (mΔθ), n = sin i / sin r) must not appear in Core questions.
- Check units and dimensions of every equation and every answer; check unit conversions (cm^3 ↔ m^3, g ↔ kg, kW h ↔ J, °C ↔ K, min ↔ s).
- Check significant figures against the data given (Section 4) and that the final answer has a unit.
- Check that any value candidates are expected to know matches Section 2 exactly (g ≈ 9.8 m / s^2, 3.0 × 10^8 m / s, 9.5 × 10^15 m, 2.2 × 10^-18 per second, 330–350 m / s, 20 Hz–20 000 Hz, 20 kHz, −273 °C).
- Check that definitions in answers match the syllabus wording (e.g. "speed as distance travelled per unit time", "density as mass per unit volume", "e.m.f. as the electrical work done by a source in moving a unit charge around a complete circuit", "p.d. as the work done by a unit charge passing through a component", "half-life ... the time taken for half the nuclei of that isotope in any sample to decay").
- Check that command words are used with the syllabus meanings (p. 55), and that "Sketch" is not used for essential-diagram content.
- Check any partial-diagram question is fully solvable from the words/table alone.

## 7. Anything else

- Command words (p. 55): Calculate, Comment, Compare, Deduce, Define, Describe, Determine, Explain, Give, Identify, Justify, Predict, Sketch, State, Suggest — use only with the meanings given.
- Questions may require candidates to apply material to unfamiliar contexts and to apply knowledge from one area of the syllabus to another (AO1, p. 10); AO2 questions may be based on unfamiliar information (p. 10).
- Paper formats (pp. 9, 42–43): Papers 1/2 are 40 four-option multiple-choice items (45 min, 40 marks); Papers 3/4 are short-answer and structured questions (1 h 15 min, 80 marks); Papers 5/6 test AO3 only (practical skills; 40 marks).
- Practical skills (pp. 43–45) are tested on Papers 5/6 and are not numbered learning points; the numbered "Describe an experiment…" points in the subject content are in scope for theory-style questions.
- Language of measurement terms (p. 45) are for correct use; candidates are not required to recall the definitions.
