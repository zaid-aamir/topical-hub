# 4PH1 Physics — subject rules (DRAFT, from the specification only)

Source: Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Physics (4PH1), Specification Issue 4 (September 2024), first teaching September 2017, first examination June 2019. Page numbers are the printed page numbers (PDF page = printed page + 6). Statements are cited by `point_id` from `config/syllabus_map.json`, exactly as numbered in the specification (e.g. `1.4`, `1.25P`). Status: draft for owner checking against the PDF.

Papers (pp. 8-9, 29): the qualification is untiered and every student sits both papers. Paper 1 (4PH1/1P, 2 h, 110 marks, 61.1%) assesses content that is not in bold and has no 'P' reference. Paper 2 (4PH1/2P, 1 h 15 min, 70 marks, 38.9%) assesses all the content, including the bold 'P' statements. Pipeline `tier`: `core` = Paper 1 & 2 content (no P suffix); `supplement` = Paper 2 only (P suffix). The website labels these "Paper 1 & 2" and "Paper 2 only", never Core/Extended. A `core` question uses only statements without the P suffix; a `supplement` question uses at least one P statement.

Paper-2-only content to watch (48 statements): units Nm and kg m/s (1.2P) and J/kg °C (5.2P); momentum, force = change in momentum / time, Newton's third law, moments, centre of gravity, beams (1.25P–1.33P); all of electrostatics (2.22P–2.28P); hearing range, oscilloscopes, pitch and loudness (3.24P–3.29P); energy resources and electricity generation (4.18P, 4.19P); all of change of state and specific heat capacity (5.8P–5.14P); electromagnets, field patterns of wires/coils/solenoids, force on a moving charged particle (6.9P–6.11P); transformers (6.17P–6.20P); absolute magnitude, HR diagram (8.11P, 8.12P); all of cosmology (8.13P–8.18P). Topic 7 has no P statements.

Facts and values stated in the content (students know them; never give them):
- Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance (1.19); factors: speed, mass, road condition, reaction time (1.20).
- All EM waves travel at the same speed in free space (3.10); order of the EM spectrum by decreasing wavelength / increasing frequency, including the colours of the visible spectrum (3.11); uses (3.12) and dangers (3.13) as listed.
- Light waves are transverse (3.14); sound waves are longitudinal (3.23); law of reflection (3.15).
- Human hearing range 20–20 000 Hz (3.24P, Paper 2 only).
- Current is the rate of flow of charge (2.14); current in metals is a flow of negatively charged electrons (2.16); voltage is the energy transferred per unit charge passed and the volt is a joule per coulomb (2.20); current is conserved at a junction (2.17); voltage across parallel components is the same (2.18).
- Power is the rate of transfer of energy or the rate of doing work (4.16).
- Absolute zero is –273 °C (5.16); Kelvin temperature of a gas is proportional to the average kinetic energy of its molecules (5.19). Kelvin–Celsius conversion is required (5.17) but no conversion equation is printed: generator and verifier use T (in K) = θ (in °C) + 273.
- Specific heat capacity is the energy required to change the temperature of an object by one degree Celsius per kilogram of mass (5.12P, Paper 2 only).
- Definition of half-life (7.12); activity measured in becquerels (7.11); α, β−, γ nature and penetrating/ionising properties (7.5); effects of α, β, γ and neutron emission on atomic and mass numbers (7.7).
- Fission of U-235 produces two radioactive daughter nuclei and a small number of neutrons (7.19); fusion is the energy source for stars (7.25).
- Stages of evolution of a Sun-like star: nebula, star (main sequence), red giant, white dwarf (8.9).

## 3. Notation and units

- Use the units listed in the content (pp. 11-27): kg, m, m/s, m/s^2, N, s, N/kg (1.1, 8.1); A, C, J, Ω, s, V, W (2.1); °, Hz, m, m/s, s (3.1); kg, J, m, m/s, m/s^2, N, s, W (4.1); °C, K, J, kg, kg/m^3, m, m^2, m^3, m/s, m/s^2, N, Pa (5.1); A, V, W (6.1); Bq, cm, h, min, s (7.1).
- Paper-2-only units: Nm and kg m/s (1.2P); J/kg °C (5.2P). Do not require these in `core` questions.
- Write units as the specification does, solidus with no spaces: `m/s`, `m/s^2`, `kg/m^3`, `N/kg`, `J/kg °C`, `kg m/s`, `Nm`. Space between number and unit: `3.5 m/s^2`.
- Powers with `^` (`v^2`, `3.0 × 10^8 m/s`, `m^3`); `×` for multiplication; Unicode Greek (ρ, λ, Ω, π, Δ, α, β, γ); subscripts written inline (p1, T1, V1, Vp, Ip, Vs, Is, λ0); fractions on one line with ` / ` and brackets where needed (`a = (v − u) / t`, `F = (mv − mu) / t`).
- Use the specification's own symbols: GPE and KE (not Ep/Ek); ΔQ for change in thermal energy and ΔT for change in temperature (5.13P); E for energy transferred (2.5, 2.21); W for weight (1.18) and for work done (4.11, 4.17); p for momentum (1.25P) and for pressure (5.5); T for time period (3.6, 8.6) and Kelvin temperature (5.21); c for critical angle (3.22), speed of light (8.16P) and specific heat capacity (5.13P). Where two meanings could appear in one question, name the quantity in words.
- "voltage" is the specification's word (2.4, 2.13, 2.20); "potential difference" appears only in Appendix 6 in brackets. Use "voltage" in questions.
- Nuclide notation as `^A_Z X`, e.g. `^14_6 C` (7.2); beta particles as β− (7.4).
- Temperature scale: "Kelvin" and "Celsius" as printed (5.17); absolute zero –273 °C.
- Mathematical symbols students must understand: <, >, ∝, ~ (Appendix 4, 3A).

## 4. Calculation conventions

The specification states only the Appendix 4 skills (pp. 45-46), all ticked for Physics unless noted:
- Numbers in decimal form and standard form (1A, 1B); ratios, fractions, percentages, powers and roots (1C); estimates without a calculator (1D); sin x and sin^-1 x with x in degrees (1E).
- Use an appropriate number of significant figures (2A); arithmetic mean (2B); order-of-magnitude calculations (2I). (2E sampling and 2G mode/median are not ticked for Physics.)
- Change the subject of an equation; substitute numerical values using appropriate units for physical quantities; solve simple algebraic equations (3B-3D).
- Graphs: y = mx + c; slope and intercept of a linear graph; slope of a tangent as rate of change; area between a curve and the x-axis by counting squares (4B, 4D-4F).
- Areas of triangles and rectangles, surface areas and volumes of cubes (5C); angles in degrees (5A).
- No significant-figure or rounding rule beyond "appropriate number of significant figures" is stated. Pipeline convention (not from the specification): give final answers to 2 or 3 significant figures consistent with the data, with a unit; round only the final answer; allow ECF.
- Every numerical answer carries a unit from the lists in Section 3; efficiency may be a percentage or a decimal (4.4 prints × 100%).

## 5. Diagrams

- `essential` points are out of scope — do not set questions on them: **1.3, 1.7, 3.16, 6.10P, 8.12P** (5 of 195 points, 2.6%). See `config/diagram_coverage.md`.
- `partial` points (38): 1.5, 1.8, 1.9, 1.15, 1.21, 1.22, 1.23, 1.32P, 1.33P, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9, 2.17, 2.18, 2.19, 2.23P, 3.3, 3.15, 3.17, 3.19, 3.20, 3.25P, 3.26P, 3.27P, 4.5, 4.9, 5.4, 5.11P, 5.14P, 6.6, 6.7, 6.9P, 6.12, 6.13, 6.16, 6.17P, 7.6, 7.13. Describe the situation fully in words (positions, distances from a pivot, directions such as "into the page", angles to the normal) or give a Markdown data table instead of a graph (e.g. `time / s` against `velocity / m/s`). Never write "see the diagram".
- Circuits: describe component by component using Appendix 8 names (e.g. "a 12 V battery in series with an ammeter, a fixed resistor and a lamp; a voltmeter is connected across the lamp").
- Graph-based points (1.8, 1.9, 1.23, 2.9, 5.11P, 7.13): give the data as a table; gradients and areas are worked from tabulated values or straight-line sections described in words.
- Do not use the command words Draw, Sketch, Plot, Add/Label or Complete (for a diagram) — they need a drawn answer. "Complete the table" is allowed.
- Practical statements (`practical: ...`) are in scope through method, variables, safety, results tables, analysis and evaluation (p. 30).

## 6. Subject-specific checks for the verifier

- Every equation used is one of the specification's relationships (Sections 1-2). Equations not in the specification (e.g. s = ut + ½at^2, Q = mL, F = mv^2 / r, R = R1 + R2, 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2, P = I^2R, Ek/Ep forms) must not be needed; if used at all, they must be stated in full in the question.
- "Know and use" relationships (Section 2) are never given in the question, in any form or rearrangement (Appendix 7). "Use the relationship" equations (Section 1 table) are stated in the question when needed (draft rule).
- Paper check: a `core` question uses only statements, relationships and units without the P suffix. In particular, momentum (p = m × v), F = (mv − mu) / t, moments, electrostatics, specific heat capacity (ΔQ = m × c × ΔT), changes of state, transformers (turns ratio, VpIp = VsIs), energy resources, absolute magnitude, HR diagram, red-shift and Δλ / λ0 = v / c, Nm, kg m/s and J/kg °C must not appear in `core` questions.
- 1.19 stopping distance and 2.19 (two resistive components in series) are in `core`; 2.19 needs current, voltage and resistance of exactly two series components — combined resistance R1 + R2 is not printed as a relationship, so state it if a question needs it, or keep to V = I × R for each component (owner to confirm).
- Every numerical value the question needs (g, c, speed of sound, specific heat capacity, density, etc.) is stated in the question — the specification supplies none.
- Check units and dimensions of every equation and every answer; check conversions (cm^3 ↔ m^3, g ↔ kg, min/h ↔ s, °C ↔ K, kJ ↔ J).
- Kelvin: gas-law questions (5.21, 5.22) use Kelvin temperatures; T (K) = θ (°C) + 273.
- Definitions in mark schemes match the specification wording: power "the rate of transfer of energy or the rate of doing work" (4.16); current "the rate of flow of charge" (2.14); voltage "the energy transferred per unit charge passed" (2.20); elastic behaviour "the ability of a material to recover its original shape after the forces causing deformation have been removed" (1.24); specific heat capacity (5.12P); stopping distance (1.19); nuclear fusion "the creation of larger nuclei resulting in a loss of mass from smaller nuclei, accompanied by a release of energy" (7.24).
- Command words are used with their Appendix 5 meanings (pp. 47-48); "Determine" answers have a quantitative element; "Show that" questions give the value to be verified; MCQs use What / Why / Which.
- A `partial` question is fully solvable from the words or table alone.
- Wording: "Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1)"; never "Cambridge", "IGCSE 0625", "Core" or "Extended" in student-facing text.

## 7. Anything else

- Question styles (pp. 8-9, 29): both papers use "a combination of different question styles, including multiple-choice questions, short-answer questions, calculations and extended open-response questions". The specification does not give the number of multiple-choice items or a fixed tariff for extended open-response questions (no "6-mark" rule is stated). Questions may come from any topic area across the specification; Paper 2 bold statements "cover some sub-topics in greater depth".
- Calculators are allowed on all papers (p. 30).
- Experimental skills are assessed in the written papers (p. 30): solving problems in a practical context; planning investigations and selecting techniques; describing methods including safe practice; making and recording measurements with appropriate precision; identifying independent, dependent and control variables; analysing and interpreting data; communicating findings with technical language, calculations and graphs; assessing reliability; evaluating data and methods (accuracy, validity).
- AO weightings (p. 31): AO1 38–42%, AO2 38–42%, AO3 19–21%. Some questions are "more problem-solving in style" and apply concepts "to a range of situations" (p. 5).
- "The word 'including' in the content specifies the detail of what must be covered"; examples ("such as", "e.g.") are illustrative only — questions may use other examples (p. 1).
- Appendix 6 (p. 49) suggested practicals are not specification statements; they may inspire practical contexts but must be answerable from mapped statements.
- Command words (Appendix 5, pp. 47-48): Add/Label, Calculate, Comment on, Complete, Deduce, Describe, Design, Determine, Discuss, Draw, Estimate, Evaluate, Explain, Give/State/Name, Give a reason/reasons, Identify, Justify, Plot, Predict, Show that, Sketch, State what is meant by, Suggest; "Analyse the data/graph to explain"; What, Why, Which for multiple-choice questions.
- Assessment is in English only (p. 33).
