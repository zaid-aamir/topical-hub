## Q23
**(a)** The greater the distance of the galaxy from the Earth, the greater the red-shift. The red-shift is (approximately) proportional to distance: e.g. galaxy 1 at 120 million light-years has 0.009 and galaxy 4 at 510 million light-years has 0.038 (a distance about 4 times as great gives about 4 times the red-shift).
**(b)** The light from the galaxies is red-shifted: its wavelength is increased, which means the galaxies are moving away from the Earth. Nearly all galaxies show red-shift, whichever direction we look. The more distant galaxies have greater red-shift, so they are moving away faster. This pattern (every galaxy moving away from the others, faster the further away) is what happens when space itself is expanding, so the universe is expanding (and so was once smaller / started from a single point).
