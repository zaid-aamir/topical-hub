## Q1

**Rationale**
The watt (W) is the unit of power. Current is measured in amperes (A), voltage in volts (V) and energy in joules (J), so A, C and D are wrong. D is a common confusion: power is the rate of transfer of energy, not the energy itself.

## Q2
- answer: D

**Rationale**
A magnet attracts any magnetic material (such as iron or steel) as well as the opposite pole of another magnet, so attraction alone cannot show that the bar is a magnet. A and B are wrong because the bar need not be a magnet; B is also wrong because a north pole would repel. C is wrong because non-magnetic metals are not attracted at all.

**Worked solution**
Only repulsion proves that an object is a magnet. Attraction to a north pole happens either because the bar is a magnet with a south pole at that end, or because magnetism is induced in an unmagnetised magnetic material.

## Q3

## Q5

## Q11

**(a)** [1]
- a line showing the direction of the (magnetic) force on a north pole placed in the field (1)
- ALLOW a line showing the direction in which a plotting compass needle points OR the direction of the magnetic field at each point

**(b)** [1]

**Worked solution**
A magnetic field line shows the direction of the force that a north pole would feel at each point, which is the direction a plotting compass needle points. Indicative points, any four from:
- there is a current in the coil (when the switch is closed) (1)
- (the long) sides of the coil are at right angles to the magnetic field, so there is a force on each side (1)
- the current in one side is in the opposite direction to the current in the other side (1)

**Worked solution**
