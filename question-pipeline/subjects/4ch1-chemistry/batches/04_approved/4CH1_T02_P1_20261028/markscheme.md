## Q1

Every Group 1 metal reacts with water to form hydrogen gas and a solution of the metal hydroxide, which is alkaline. A is wrong because all three metals float on water. C is true only of potassium. D is wrong because the gas is hydrogen, not oxygen, and the solution is alkaline, not acidic.

## Q29

**(a)** [1]

**(b)** [4]
Indicative points, any four (1 each):
- while it is complete, the zinc coating is a barrier that keeps oxygen (air) and water away from the steel (1)
- zinc is more reactive than iron (1)
- when scratched, the zinc reacts OR corrodes instead of the iron (1)
- zinc loses electrons OR is oxidised in preference to iron (1)
- this is sacrificial protection (1)

**(c)** [1]
- the magnesium corrodes OR reacts OR is used up (as it protects the steel) (1)

**(d)** [2]
- copper is less reactive than iron (1)
- so copper would not corrode in place of the iron, and the iron would still rust (1)
- ALLOW: the iron would rust faster

**Worked solution**
(a) Rusting needs both oxygen and water. A layer of paint covers the iron so neither can reach it.
(b) A complete zinc layer works like paint: it keeps air and water off the steel. If the coating is scratched, the steel is exposed, but zinc is more reactive than iron. The zinc reacts with the oxygen and water instead of the iron, losing electrons in preference to it, so the iron does not rust. The zinc is sacrificed to protect the iron.
(c) The magnesium blocks protect the hull by reacting instead of the iron. As they do this the magnesium is gradually used up, so the blocks must be replaced.
(d) Sacrificial protection only works if the attached metal is more reactive than iron. Copper is less reactive than iron, so the iron would react instead of the copper and the hull would still rust.

## Q30

**(a)** [3]
- M is between iron and copper (in the reactivity series) (1)
- M does not displace iron, so M is less reactive than iron (1)
- M displaces copper, so M is more reactive than copper (1)
- ALLOW: M reacts with dilute acid but copper does not, as an alternative for the third point

**(b)** [2]
- MSO4 (1)
- MCl2 (1)

**(c)** [2]
- yes, it would react (1)
- M is more reactive than copper, so it can remove the oxygen from copper(II) oxide (1)
- ECF: from the position of M given in (a)

**Worked solution**
(a) M cannot displace iron from iron(II) sulfate, so M is less reactive than iron. M does displace copper from copper(II) sulfate, so M is more reactive than copper. Its slow reaction with acid and no reaction with cold water also fit a metal just below iron. So M is between iron and copper.
(b) Sulfate ions are SO4^2– and chloride ions are Cl^–. One M^2+ balances one SO4^2–, giving MSO4. One M^2+ needs two Cl^–, giving MCl2.
(c) A more reactive metal takes oxygen from the oxide of a less reactive metal. M is more reactive than copper, so M + copper(II) oxide → M oxide + copper.
