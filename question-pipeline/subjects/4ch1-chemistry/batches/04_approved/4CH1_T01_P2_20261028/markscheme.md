## Q7
- answer: D

**Rationale**
A Group 2 metal atom has two outer electrons and loses them, forming a 2+ ion. Metals do not gain electrons (and a negative magnesium ion does not form); protons are never lost in chemical reactions; sharing electrons is covalent bonding, not ion formation.

**(c)** [2]
- a rubidium atom has one outer electron, which it loses (to get a full outer shell) (1)
- a selenium atom has six outer electrons and gains two electrons (to get a full outer shell) (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Group 1 metals lose one electron to form 1+ ions, so rubidium forms Rb^+. Group 6 non-metals gain two electrons to form 2– ions, so selenium forms Se^2–. Two Rb^+ balance one Se^2–, so the formula is Rb2Se.

(b) Ionic compounds do not conduct when solid, because the ions cannot move, but do conduct when molten, because the ions can move. They also have high melting points because of the strong attractions between ions. K fits all of these. J conducts when solid, so it behaves like a metal. L has a low melting point and does not conduct even when molten, so it contains no ions.

(c) Rubidium is in Group 1, so its atom has one outer electron; losing it leaves a full outer shell and a charge of 1+. Selenium is in Group 6, so its atom has six outer electrons; gaining two electrons fills the outer shell and gives a charge of 2–.
