## Q1

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons between two atoms, so B is correct. A describes electron transfer in forming ionic bonds, C describes ionic bonding, and D describes intermolecular forces, which act between molecules, not within them.

## Q2
- answer: D

**Rationale**
In O2 the two oxygen atoms share two pairs of electrons, which is a double covalent bond, so D is correct. H2O and NH3 contain only single bonds (A and B), and N2 contains a triple bond (C), not a double bond.

**Worked solution**
Count the shared pairs between the bonded atoms: O–H in water is one pair, N–H in ammonia is one pair, N≡N is three pairs, and O=O is two pairs. Only oxygen has exactly two shared pairs between two atoms.

## Q4
- answer: C

**Rationale**
Metals are malleable because the layers of atoms (positive ions) can slide over each other while the metallic bonding holds them together, so C is correct. A is wrong because metals do not have covalent bonds. B is wrong because the ions stay in the lattice; only the delocalised electrons move. D is wrong because metallic bonding is strong.

**Worked solution**
In a metal, positive ions are arranged in layers surrounded by delocalised electrons. When a force is applied, the layers slide, but the delocalised electrons still attract all the ions, so the metal changes shape instead of shattering.

## Q11

**(a)** [2]
- (in graphite) each carbon atom is bonded to three other carbon atoms, so one electron per atom is delocalised (1)
- the delocalised electrons can move through the structure (and carry charge) (1)
- ALLOW: free electrons OR mobile electrons for delocalised electrons
- REJECT: ions moving (do not award the second mark)

**(b)** [4]

- the layers can slide over each other (1)
- ALLOW: hexagonal layers OR sheets for layers

**Worked solution**

Nothing can move without breaking many strong bonds, so diamond is very hard and is used on drill tips. In graphite, the atoms are bonded strongly within flat layers, but the forces between the layers are weak. The layers can slide over each other easily, so graphite is soft and slippery.

## Q15

**(a)** [2]
- (copper contains) delocalised electrons (1)
- which move through the metal (when a voltage is applied) (1)
- ALLOW: free OR mobile electrons
- REJECT: ions moving

**(b)** [2]
- copper(II) chloride contains ions (1)
- the ions are held in fixed positions in the (solid) lattice and cannot move (1)
- REJECT: "there are no ions in solid copper(II) chloride"
- REJECT: "electrons cannot move" alone

**(c)** [2]
- in molten copper(II) chloride the charge is carried by ions, which gain or lose electrons at the electrodes, forming new substances (copper and chlorine) (1)
- in copper wire the charge is carried by delocalised electrons, which flow through the metal without any chemical change (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) In a metal, the delocalised electrons are not attached to any one ion. When the wire is connected to a power supply, these electrons flow through the metal, which is an electric current.
(b) Copper(II) chloride is made of Cu^2+ and Cl^– ions. In the solid the ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice. They cannot move, and there are no delocalised electrons, so no charge can flow.
(c) In molten copper(II) chloride, the ions can move. The Cu^2+ ions move to the cathode and gain electrons to form copper; the Cl^– ions move to the anode and lose electrons to form chlorine. The compound is broken down: this is electrolysis. In a copper wire, only electrons move. The copper ions stay where they are, so the copper is not changed.
