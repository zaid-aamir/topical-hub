## Q1
- answer: B

**Rationale**
Thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate needs heat energy to be taken in continuously, so it is endothermic. Combustion (A), neutralisation (C) and the reaction of sodium with water (D) all give out heat energy, so they are exothermic.

**Worked solution**
An endothermic reaction takes in heat energy from the surroundings. Calcium carbonate only breaks down into calcium oxide and carbon dioxide while it is being strongly heated, because the reaction needs that energy. Burning a fuel, neutralising an acid and reacting sodium with water all make their surroundings hotter, so they are exothermic.

Eventually the two rates become equal. From then on HI is made as fast as it breaks down, so the amounts of all three gases stay constant.
