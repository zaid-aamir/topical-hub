- polymer R is inert OR not biodegradable, so it stays in the landfill site for a very long time (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) The double bond in CH2=C(CH3)2 opens. One carbon carries two H atoms and the other carries two CH3 groups, giving –[CH2–C(CH3)2]–n.
(b) The –COOH groups of the acid react with the –OH groups of the diol, losing water. The acid part –CO–CH2CH2–CO– is joined through ester links to the diol part –O–CH2CH2CH2–O–, so the repeat unit is –[CO–CH2CH2–CO–O–CH2CH2CH2–O]–n.
(c) Biopolyesters are biodegradable: microorganisms break them down over time, so they do not build up in landfill. Addition polymers like R are unreactive and are not broken down, so they stay in landfill for hundreds of years.

## Q30

**(a)** [5]
Indicative points, any five (1 each):
- poly(ethene) forms by addition polymerisation (1)
- the polyester forms by condensation polymerisation (1)
- the ethene monomer contains a C=C double bond, which opens (1)
- the polyester monomers each have two functional groups, –COOH and –OH (1)
- poly(ethene) is made from one type of monomer but a polyester is made from two different monomers, a dicarboxylic acid and a diol (1)
- addition polymerisation gives the polymer as the only product (1)
- condensation polymerisation also gives water (a small molecule) (1)

**(b)** [1]
- both join many small monomer molecules to form long-chain molecules (1)
- ALLOW: both form polymers OR both produce large molecules with a repeat unit

**Worked solution**
(a) Poly(ethene) is made by addition polymerisation. Ethene has a C=C double bond; one bond of it opens and thousands of ethene molecules join. Only one type of monomer is needed and the polymer is the only product. A polyester is made by condensation polymerisation. Two different monomers are used, a dicarboxylic acid (two –COOH groups) and a diol (two –OH groups). Each time a –COOH group reacts with an –OH group, an ester link forms and a molecule of water is given off, so there are two products: the polymer and water.
(b) In both types, many small monomer molecules join together to make very long chain molecules (polymers) built from a repeat unit.
