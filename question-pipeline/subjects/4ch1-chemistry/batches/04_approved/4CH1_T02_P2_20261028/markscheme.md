## Q1

**Rationale**
Gold is very unreactive, so it does not combine readily with other elements and is found as the uncombined (native) metal. Aluminium (A), iron (B) and zinc (D) are more reactive, so they are found combined in compounds in ores.

**Worked solution**

## Q2
- answer: A

**Rationale**
In a pure metal the atoms are the same size and form regular layers that slide easily. Atoms of a different size in an alloy distort the layers, so it is harder for them to slide. B is wrong because the bonding in an alloy is still metallic, not covalent. C is wrong because the delocalised electrons do not explain hardness. D is wrong because weaker forces would make the metal softer, not harder.

**Worked solution**
Hardness depends on how easily the layers of atoms can slide over each other. Adding atoms of another element, which are a different size, breaks up the regular pattern. The layers can no longer slide past each other easily, so the alloy is harder than the pure metal.

## Q29

- ALLOW: copper chloride
- REJECT: iron(II) bromide

**(b)** [1]
- Cu(OH)2 (1)

**(c)** [2]

- the orange-brown precipitate shows that iron(III) ions are present (1)

**(d)** [1]
- to remove carbonate ions, which would also give a precipitate with silver nitrate (1)
- ALLOW: to stop other ions (such as carbonate or hydroxide) giving a precipitate OR to prevent a false positive result

**Worked solution**
(a) X: a blue precipitate with sodium hydroxide means Cu^2+, and a white precipitate with acidified silver nitrate means Cl^–, so X is copper(II) chloride. (d) Carbonate ions also form a precipitate with silver ions. The nitric acid reacts with any carbonate ions first, so a precipitate can only come from a halide ion.

## Q30

**(a)** [3]
- add sodium hydroxide solution and warm (1)
- test the gas with damp red litmus paper (1)
- the litmus paper turns blue (1)
- ALLOW: damp universal indicator paper turns blue OR purple

**(b)** [2]
- add dilute hydrochloric acid and then barium chloride solution (1)
- a white precipitate forms (1)
- ALLOW: acidified barium chloride solution
- REJECT: sulfuric acid as the acid

**(c)(i)** [1]
- potassium nitrate contains no ammonium ions, so no ammonia (gas) is given off (1)

**(c)(ii)** [1]
- potassium hydroxide is soluble, so no precipitate forms (1)
- ALLOW: there is no visible change OR no precipitate forms

**Worked solution**
(a) Ammonium ions react with warm sodium hydroxide solution to give ammonia gas. Ammonia is alkaline, so it turns damp red litmus paper blue.
(b) Add dilute hydrochloric acid, then barium chloride solution. Sulfate ions form insoluble barium sulfate, a white precipitate.
(c)(i) The test in (a) works only because ammonium ions make ammonia. Potassium nitrate has no ammonium ions, so no ammonia forms and the litmus stays red.
(c)(ii) The sodium hydroxide test works by forming an insoluble metal hydroxide precipitate. Potassium hydroxide is soluble, so nothing is seen. A flame test (lilac flame) is used to identify potassium ions instead.
