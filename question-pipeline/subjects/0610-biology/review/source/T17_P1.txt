# Planned split: all 30 questions on 17.1 (12 eligible points).
M|17.1.1|What material forms chromosomes and carries genetic information?|DNA|starch|fat|cellulose|Chromosomes are made of DNA. Starch and cellulose are carbohydrates, and fat is a lipid; none is the chromosome's genetic material.
M|17.1.2|Which statement defines a gene?|a length of DNA that codes for a protein|a whole cell containing two nuclei|any protein found in a chromosome|a membrane surrounding a nucleus|A gene is a length of DNA coding for a protein. It is not a cell, membrane or the protein it encodes.
M|17.1.3|Two versions of one gene occur at the same position on corresponding chromosomes. What are these versions called?|alleles|ribosomes|gametes|enzymes|Alleles are alternative forms of one gene. Ribosomes make proteins, gametes are reproductive cells, and enzymes are proteins with catalytic roles.
M|17.1.4|Which normal combination of sex chromosomes occurs in a human male body cell?|XY|XX|YY|X alone|Human males normally have XY and females XX in body cells. A single X is a gamete complement, not the normal male body-cell pair.
M|17.1.4|An egg containing an X chromosome is fertilised by a sperm containing an X chromosome. What is the expected sex-chromosome combination in the zygote?|XX|XY|YY|XXX|One X from each gamete gives XX. The sperm in this example contributes no Y and each normal gamete contributes one sex chromosome.
M|17.1.5|Which feature of a gene most directly determines the amino-acid sequence of its protein product?|the order of its bases|the position of the cell in a tissue|the amount of starch in the cell|the colour of the nucleus|The order of DNA bases determines the order of amino acids. Cell position and starch do not specify that sequence.
M|17.1.6|Two proteins contain different amino-acid sequences. Which consequence is most directly expected?|They may fold into different shapes.|They must contain identical active sites.|They must be DNA molecules.|They must have the same function.|Different amino-acid sequences can produce different folding and shapes. Identical function or active sites is not guaranteed.
M|17.1.7|A cell makes a different membrane carrier after a change in a gene. How can this affect cell function?|It can alter the movement of a substance across the membrane.|It changes the membrane into a chromosome.|It prevents the cell containing any DNA.|It turns every cell protein into the same carrier.|DNA controls production of proteins including carriers. A changed carrier may alter transport without removing DNA or changing every protein.
M|17.1.8|Which molecule carries a copy of a gene's information from the nucleus to the cytoplasm?|mRNA|cellulose|glycogen|a whole chromosome leaving the nucleus|mRNA copies genetic information and moves to the cytoplasm. Chromosomes normally remain in the nucleus during this process.
M|17.1.8|Where are amino acids assembled into a protein during protein synthesis?|ribosome|cell wall|vacuole membrane|chromosome surface in the nucleus|Ribosomes assemble amino acids in the order specified by mRNA. The other locations are not the site of protein assembly.
M|17.1.9|A skin cell and a muscle cell from the same person make different proteins. Which explanation is best?|Different genes are expressed in the two cell types.|All their genes must be different.|The muscle cell has no chromosomes.|The skin cell has no DNA.|Most body cells have the same genes but express different subsets. Specialisation does not normally require loss of all DNA or entirely different genomes.
M|17.1.10|A nucleus contains one complete set of chromosomes. How is it described?|haploid|diploid|a protein|a pair of gametes|Haploid means one set of chromosomes. Diploid means two sets; neither term describes a protein or a pair of cells.
M|17.1.11|A diploid nucleus has which arrangement?|two sets of chromosomes|one chromosome in total|no genetic material|one set of amino acids|Diploid nuclei contain two chromosome sets. This refers to chromosome sets, not a fixed total of one chromosome or amino acids.
M|17.1.12|How many chromosomes are normally present in a human diploid body-cell nucleus?|46|23|44|92|There are 23 pairs, giving 46 chromosomes. Twenty-three is the normal haploid number; 44 counts only the autosomes.
M|17.1.8|During protein synthesis, what determines the order in which a ribosome assembles amino acids?|the base sequence of the mRNA passing through it|the order of cells in the tissue|the concentration of oxygen alone|the position of the ribosome in the cell membrane|The mRNA base sequence specifies the amino-acid sequence. Oxygen availability can affect metabolism but does not encode the sequence.
S|17.1.1,17.1.2,17.1.3|An inherited condition is associated with a gene on a chromosome.|State what chromosomes are made of and define a gene.|Chromosomes are made of DNA.~A gene is a length of DNA that codes for a protein.|Define an allele and explain why two alleles of this gene can differ.|An allele is an alternative form of a gene.~Different alleles have differences in their DNA sequence.
S|17.1.4|The parents of a child have normal human sex-chromosome combinations.|State the sex chromosomes in each parent's body cells.|The mother has XX.~The father has XY.|Explain why either an XX or an XY zygote can be produced.|Eggs each contain an X chromosome.~Sperm contain either X or Y, giving XX or XY after fertilisation.
S|17.1.5,17.1.6|Two alleles produce proteins with different shapes.|Describe the link between DNA and the amino-acid sequence.|The DNA base sequence differs between the alleles.~The base sequence determines the order of amino acids in the protein.|Explain how this difference can affect an enzyme's activity.|A changed amino-acid sequence can alter the protein's shape.~A changed active-site shape may reduce binding of the usual substrate.
S|17.1.7|A cell's gene controls production of an enzyme involved in one metabolic reaction.|Explain why this gene can affect the cell's metabolism.|The gene controls production of a protein, the enzyme.~The enzyme catalyses a reaction, so its production affects that reaction.|State two other kinds of protein through which DNA can control cell function.|Membrane carrier proteins.~Receptors for neurotransmitters.
S|17.1.8|A cell begins making a protein coded by one of its nuclear genes.|Describe the events that carry the information to the cytoplasm.|An mRNA copy of the gene is made in the nucleus.~The mRNA moves from the nucleus to the cytoplasm.|Describe how the information is used there.|The mRNA passes through a ribosome.~The ribosome assembles amino acids in the sequence specified by the mRNA.
S|17.1.9|A pancreatic cell makes a protein that a nerve cell from the same person does not make.|Explain why this does not mean that the gene is absent from the nerve cell.|Most body cells in one individual contain the same genes.~A gene can be present without being expressed.|Explain how gene expression contributes to cell specialisation.|Different cells express different genes.~They produce the particular proteins needed for their specific functions.
S|17.1.10,17.1.11|A plant has 16 chromosomes in a normal diploid body-cell nucleus.|Define diploid and give the chromosome number in a haploid nucleus of this plant.|Diploid means containing two sets of chromosomes.~The haploid number is 8.|Explain why the haploid nucleus has this number.|A haploid nucleus contains one set of chromosomes.~One set is half the diploid number, so 16 divided by 2 is 8.
S|17.1.12,17.1.10|A laboratory compares normal human body-cell and gamete nuclei.|State the chromosome number in each type of nucleus.|A body-cell nucleus contains 46 chromosomes.~A gamete nucleus contains 23 chromosomes.|Describe their chromosome sets using the terms haploid and diploid.|The body-cell nucleus is diploid with two sets.~The gamete nucleus is haploid with one set.
S|17.1.8|A student claims that a gene leaves the nucleus and becomes a protein.|Correct the claim about what leaves the nucleus.|The gene remains in the nucleus.~An mRNA copy moves to the cytoplasm instead.|Correct the claim about how the protein is formed.|A ribosome assembles amino acids to form the protein.~The sequence is specified by the mRNA rather than DNA being converted directly into protein.
S|17.1.7,17.1.9|A receptor protein is abundant in one cell type but absent in another cell type of the same organism.|Explain how DNA can control the response to a chemical signal.|DNA controls production of the receptor protein.~Whether the receptor is present affects the cell's ability to detect the signal.|Explain why the two cell types can differ despite containing the same genes.|The receptor gene is expressed in one cell type.~It is not expressed, or is expressed much less, in the other cell type.
S|17.1.4|In a family, the first two children have normal XX sex chromosomes.|State the two possible sex chromosomes in the father's sperm.|Some sperm contain X.~Other sperm contain Y.|Explain why this history does not make an XY next child certain.|Fertilisation by an X-bearing or Y-bearing sperm is a new event.~Each outcome remains approximately equally likely regardless of earlier children.
S|17.1.5,17.1.6|A protein used as a membrane carrier has a different amino-acid order in two individuals.|State the genetic feature that specifies this order.|The sequence of bases in the gene specifies amino-acid order.~The two individuals may have different alleles of that gene.|Explain why the carrier might not work equally well in both individuals.|Different amino-acid sequences can produce different protein shapes.~A change in shape may affect binding or transport of the usual substance.
S|17.1.8|A protein contains the amino acids alanine, glycine and serine in a specific order.|Explain why a supply of these amino acids alone does not specify the correct protein.|The amino acids could be assembled in different sequences.~The correct order must be specified by genetic information.|Describe the role of mRNA and the ribosome in obtaining the correct sequence.|The mRNA base sequence supplies the sequence information.~The ribosome reads that information while assembling the amino acids.
S|17.1.10,17.1.11,17.1.12|Two species have different normal diploid chromosome numbers: species P has 10 and species Q has 28.|Calculate their haploid chromosome numbers.|P has 10 divided by 2, giving 5.~Q has 28 divided by 2, giving 14.|Explain why a larger chromosome number does not change the meaning of diploid.|Diploid describes two complete sets, regardless of the number in each set.~Both species have chromosome pairs, although the number of pairs differs.
S|17.1.1,17.1.2,17.1.7|A change in one gene reduces the amount of a particular enzyme made in a cell.|Describe the relationship between chromosome, DNA and gene.|A chromosome is made of DNA.~A gene is a length of that DNA coding for a protein.|Explain how a change in this gene can alter the cell's function.|The changed gene can alter production of the enzyme protein.~Less enzyme may reduce the rate of the metabolic reaction it catalyses.
