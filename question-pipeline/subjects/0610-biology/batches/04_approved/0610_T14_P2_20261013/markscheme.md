## Q1
- answer: B

**Rationale**
The syllabus describes a hormone as a chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs. Hormones are not enzymes and do not travel along neurones, electrical impulses belong to nervous control, and hormones affect only their specific target organs, not every organ equally.

## Q2

**Rationale**
In a 'fight or flight' situation adrenaline increases the breathing rate, increases the heart rate and increases the pupil diameter. Each of the other rows shows at least one of these as a decrease.

**Worked solution**
Think about what helps the man escape: faster breathing brings in more oxygen, a faster heart rate carries oxygen and glucose to the muscles more quickly, and wider pupils let more light in so he can see better. All three increase.

## Q3

**Rationale**
Hormones travel in the blood, so hormonal control acts more slowly than nervous control, which uses electrical impulses along neurones. Hormonal effects last longer because the hormone stays in the blood for some time. The other rows get the speed, the duration, or both, the wrong way round.

## Q6

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment. Coordinating responses to external stimuli is the role of the nervous system, removing metabolic waste is excretion, and homeostasis is about conditions inside the body, not the surroundings.

## Q8
- answer: C

**Rationale**
In negative feedback, a change away from the set point causes a response that reverses the change, bringing the condition back to the set point. Moving the condition further away is the opposite of negative feedback, the set point itself stays the same, and no correction is needed when the condition is already at the set point.

## Q9
- answer: A

**Rationale**
When blood glucose falls below the set point, the pancreas secretes more glucagon, which causes the liver to convert glycogen to glucose and release it into the blood. Insulin is secreted when blood glucose is too high, and converting glucose to glycogen would lower blood glucose even further.

**Worked solution**
Link each hormone to its job: insulin lowers blood glucose (liver stores glucose as glycogen); glucagon raises blood glucose (liver breaks glycogen down to glucose). After several hours without food the problem is low glucose, so the answer must be glucagon with glycogen being broken down.

## Q10

Rapid, repeated muscle contractions are shivering; the extra respiration in the muscles releases heat. Sweating cools the body, dilation of arterioles to the skin increases heat loss, and hairs lying flat trap less air, so less insulation; all three would lower body temperature further.

## Q11
- answer: B

**Rationale**
In vasoconstriction the arterioles supplying the skin surface capillaries narrow, so less warm blood flows near the skin surface and less heat is lost by radiation. Capillaries do not move, wider arterioles describe vasodilation which increases heat loss, and vasoconstriction is about reducing heat loss from blood, not about sweat production.

**Worked solution**
Many students wrongly say that the capillaries move down. They do not move at all. What changes is the width of the arterioles that feed them: narrow arterioles mean little blood reaches the surface capillaries, so less heat is carried to the skin surface to be lost.

Phototropism is growth towards or away from the direction of a light source; shoots bending towards a window are growing towards the light. Roots growing down and shoots growing up in the dark are responses to gravity (gravitropism), and coiling around a stick is a response to touch, not light.

## Q14
- answer: A

**Rationale**
In the dark the only directional stimulus is gravity. Roots grow towards gravity and shoots grow away from it, so every root curves to grow downwards and every shoot curves to grow upwards whatever position the seed started in. Roots and shoots do not just carry on in their starting direction, roots do not grow upwards, and in a dark box there is no light to make shoots grow towards one side.

## Q15
- answer: D

**Rationale**
Auxin is unequally distributed in response to light, with more on the shaded (right) side. Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so cells on the right side elongate more and the shoot bends towards the light on the left. Auxin does not collect on the lit side, it stimulates elongation rather than faster division, and if the right side grows longer the shoot cannot bend to the right.

**Worked solution**
Step 1: light from the left means the right side is shaded, so more auxin is found on the right. Step 2: auxin makes cells elongate, so the right side grows longer. Step 3: when one side grows longer than the other, the shoot curves away from the longer side, so it bends to the left, towards the light.

## Q22

**(a)** [2]
- insulin concentration decreased (from 14 to 5 arbitrary units) (1)
- glucagon concentration increased (from 20 to 66 arbitrary units) (1)
- ALLOW: both levelled off / changed very little from 8 hours

**(b)** [3]
- glucagon is carried in the blood to the liver (1)
- liver converts glycogen to glucose (1)
- glucose is released into the blood (1)
- blood glucose concentration is kept at / near the set point (1)
- max 3
- REJECT: glucagon converted to glucose / glucagon converted to glycogen

**(c)** [1]
- less glucose is removed from the blood / less glucose is converted to glycogen (so blood glucose does not fall further) (1)
- ALLOW: so insulin does not cancel out the effect of glucagon

**Worked solution**
As time without food increased, the insulin concentration fell from 14 to 5 arbitrary units, while the glucagon concentration rose from 20 to 66 arbitrary units. Glucagon travels in the blood to the liver, where it causes stored glycogen to be converted into glucose. This glucose is released into the blood, so the blood glucose concentration levels off at about 83 mg per 100 cm^3 instead of falling further. Insulin has the opposite effect: it causes the liver to take glucose out of the blood and store it as glycogen. Keeping insulin low while food is not available means glucose is not removed from the blood when it is needed by the body's cells.

## Q23

**(a)** [3]
- insulin injections (1)
- regular measuring / monitoring of the blood glucose concentration (1)
- control of diet, e.g. limiting carbohydrate / sugar intake or eating regular meals (1)
- ALLOW: insulin pump for insulin injections
- ALLOW: adjusting insulin dose or food intake when exercising (1)

- by protease (enzymes) into amino acids (1)
- so it would no longer work / would not be absorbed into the blood as insulin (1)

**Worked solution**
People with Type 1 diabetes cannot make enough insulin, so their blood glucose concentration can rise too high. Treatment involves injecting insulin, regularly checking the blood glucose concentration so the dose can be adjusted, and controlling the diet, especially the amount of carbohydrate eaten. Insulin is a protein, so if it were swallowed, protease enzymes in the stomach and small intestine would digest it into amino acids. It would then no longer work as insulin, which is why it is injected instead.
