## Q19

**Worked solution**
A mutation is a genetic change, a change in the DNA of a gene or in a chromosome. ## Q23
**(a)** [2]
- A gene mutation can create it. (1)
- ALLOW: mutation, unqualified, for this mark
- A gene mutation is a random change in the DNA base sequence. (1)

**(b)** [2]
- Meiosis produces gametes with different genetic combinations. (1)
- Random mating or random fertilisation brings together different existing alleles. (1)
- ALLOW: independent assortment / crossing over as detail of meiosis

**Worked solution**
Only mutation can create a base sequence that did not exist before: a gene mutation is a random change in the base sequence of DNA, which can give a new allele. Sexual reproduction cannot make new alleles, but it shuffles existing ones. Meiosis gives each gamete a different combination of the parent's alleles, random mating decides which two individuals breed, and random fertilisation decides which gametes fuse. Together these produce offspring with new combinations of alleles, increasing variation in the population.

## Q24
**(a)** [2]
- Rolled leaves trap moist air and reduce air movement near stomata. (1)
- Sunken stomata retain humid air, reducing the water-vapour concentration gradient. (1)
- ALLOW: reduces the water potential gradient / diffusion gradient; air next to stomata becomes saturated

**(b)** [2]
- A thick cuticle reduces evaporation from the leaf surface. (1)
- Storage tissue retains water for use when water uptake is limited. (1)
- ALLOW: cuticle is waxy / waterproof; storage allows survival between rainfalls

**Worked solution**
Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the leaf, mainly through the stomata, and it is faster when the air outside is dry and moving. Rolled leaves enclose the stomata so that still, humid air is trapped inside the roll, and sunken pits do the same on a smaller scale: the air in the pit becomes saturated with water vapour, so the concentration gradient between the inside of the leaf and the air just outside the stomata is small and diffusion is slow. A thick waxy cuticle is waterproof, so very little water evaporates through the leaf surface itself. Water-storage tissue holds water taken up during rare rainfall so the plant can keep its cells turgid and keep photosynthesising during long dry periods.

## Q26
**(a)** [2]
- Predators are less likely to detect and eat dark beetles. (1)
- ALLOW: camouflage; light beetles are eaten more
- More dark beetles survive to reproduce. (1)

**(b)** [2]
- Surviving dark beetles pass their alleles to offspring. (1)
- The alleles producing the advantageous colour become more common. (1)
- ALLOW: proportion of dark beetles rises each generation; natural selection

**Worked solution**
On dark ground, dark beetles are camouflaged, so predators find and eat fewer of them than light beetles. More dark beetles therefore survive long enough to reproduce. Because colour is inherited, the surviving dark beetles pass on the allele for dark colour to their offspring, while fewer light beetles breed. In each generation the proportion of dark beetles rises, so the frequency of the dark form in the population increases. This is natural selection.

## Q27
**(a)** [2]
- Select parents with high milk yield or a family history of high yield. (1)
- ALLOW: select males whose mothers or sisters have high milk yield
- Cross the selected individuals. (1)

**(b)** [2]
- Select offspring that show high milk yield. (1)
- Breed the selected offspring and repeat over many generations. (1)
- ALLOW: cross selected offspring with each other or with other high-yielding goats

**Worked solution**
Selective breeding starts by choosing parents that show the desired feature: the breeder selects female goats with the highest milk yield (and males whose mothers or sisters are high yielders, since males do not produce milk). These selected goats are crossed to produce the next generation. The offspring are then assessed, and only those with the highest milk yield are kept and bred together. Repeating this selection and crossing over many generations gradually increases the average milk yield of the herd.

## Q28
**(a)** [2]
- Susceptible bacteria are killed or their reproduction is inhibited. (1)
- Resistant bacteria survive more successfully. (1)
- ALLOW: population size falls sharply

**(b)** [2]
- Surviving resistant bacteria reproduce. (1)
- Their offspring inherit resistance, increasing its frequency in the population. (1)
- ALLOW: less competition for resources; natural selection; a resistant strain develops

**Worked solution**
Antibiotics kill bacteria or stop them reproducing, so when treatment starts most of the population, the susceptible bacteria, die and the population falls sharply. The few bacteria that already carried a resistance allele (from a random mutation) are not killed. After treatment these survivors reproduce rapidly, with little competition from the susceptible bacteria that have died, and pass the resistance allele to their offspring. The proportion of resistant bacteria therefore increases until the population is mostly resistant. The antibiotic did not create the resistance; it selected for bacteria that already had it, which is natural selection.

## Q29
**(a)** [2]
- Inherited drought-tolerant variants survive and reproduce more successfully. (1)
- ALLOW: there is variation in drought tolerance; less tolerant plants die
- Their alleles become more common over many generations. (1)

**(b)** [2]
- Environmental conditions determine which wild variants reproduce most successfully. (1)
- The farmer chooses which cultivated plants reproduce for seed yield. (1)
- ALLOW: natural selection in the wild population, artificial selection in the farmed population

**Worked solution**
In the wild population there is genetic variation: some grass plants tolerate drought better than others. During a drought the less tolerant plants die, while the tolerant ones survive and reproduce, passing on their alleles. Over many generations the alleles for drought tolerance become more common and the population becomes more suited to its environment; this process is adaptation by natural selection. In the farmer's field, survival in the wild no longer decides reproductive success. The farmer chooses which plants provide seed for the next generation on the basis of seed yield, so it is human choice, artificial selection, that determines which alleles are passed on.

## Q30
**(a)** [2]
- Food and other resources are limited. (1)
- Many young compete for those resources and some die. (1)
- ALLOW: named resources (food, oxygen, space); predation

**(b)** [2]
- Individuals already differ in inherited features (variation arising from mutation, meiosis and random fertilisation). (1)
- Those with advantageous features survive and leave more offspring; an individual cannot choose or change its alleles. (1)
- ALLOW: the environment determines which variants survive

**Worked solution**
A pond has limited food, oxygen and space, so if many more young fish are produced than the pond can support, they must compete for these resources and many die: a struggle for survival. Which fish survive depends on inherited differences that already exist in the population, such as faster swimming or better camouflage, which arose by mutation and were shuffled by meiosis and random fertilisation. A fish cannot decide to change its alleles; the environment simply allows those with advantageous features to survive, reproduce and pass those alleles on.
