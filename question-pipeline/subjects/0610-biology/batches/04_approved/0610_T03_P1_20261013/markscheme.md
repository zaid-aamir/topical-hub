## Q18

**(a)** [3]
- soil water has a higher water potential than the cell sap / cytoplasm of the root hair cell (1)
- water moves into the root hair cell by osmosis (1)
- through the partially permeable (cell) membrane (1)
- ALLOW: from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution

**(b)** [2]
- the salt lowers the water potential of the soil water, to below that of the root cells (1)
- so water does not enter / water leaves the root cells by osmosis, so the cells lose turgor (and the plant wilts) (1)

**(c)** [2]
- in both, water enters by osmosis because distilled water has a higher water potential than the cell contents (1)
- red blood cells have no cell wall, but the plant cell wall resists the pressure / stops the plant cell bursting (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Soil water is usually a dilute solution, so it has a higher water potential than the cell sap inside the root hair cell. Water moves by osmosis from the higher water potential (soil) to the lower water potential (cell) through the partially permeable cell membrane.
(b) When salt builds up, the soil water becomes more concentrated and its water potential falls. If it falls below the water potential of the root cells, water no longer enters the roots and may even leave them by osmosis. The plant cannot replace water it loses, its cells lose turgor and the plant wilts, even though the soil is wet.
(c) Distilled water has the highest water potential, so water moves into both kinds of cell by osmosis. A red blood cell has only a cell membrane, which stretches until it bursts. A plant cell has a strong cell wall; as the cell swells, the wall pushes back (turgor pressure) and stops it bursting.
