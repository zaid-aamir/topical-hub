## Q6

- first pair of statements uses one feature, with opposite statements, to divide the four fruits into two groups (1)

- each of E, F, G and H appears once at the end of a branch and is correctly identified (1)
- example: 1 fruit is fleshy, go to 2 / fruit is not fleshy, G; 2 fruit has many seeds, F / fruit has one seed, go to 3; 3 skin is hairy, E / skin is smooth, H
- ALLOW any order of features that works, e.g. starting with surface of skin or number of seeds
- ALLOW keys written as numbered statements, a table or a branching outline
- REJECT a pair of statements that are not about the same feature, e.g. "fruit is fleshy / fruit has many seeds" (no credit for that pair)

**(b)** [2]
- colour varies between fruits of the same type / colour changes as the fruit ripens (1)
- so fruits of the same type could be identified as different fruits / the key would give a wrong identification / a key needs features that are the same in all fruits of one type (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Four fruits need three pairs of statements, because each pair splits a group into two. Pick a feature that separates one fruit from the other three: G is the only fruit that is not fleshy. So step 1 is "fruit is fleshy, go to 2" or "fruit is not fleshy, G". That leaves E, F and H, which are all fleshy. F is the only one with many seeds, so step 2 is "many seeds, F" or "one seed, go to 3". That leaves E and H; E has a hairy skin and H has a smooth skin, so step 3 separates them. Each pair of statements must be about one feature and must be opposites, so that every fruit fits exactly one statement. Other orders work too, as long as every fruit ends up on its own.

- to produce fertile offspring (1)

**(d)** [1]

**Worked solution**
(a) In the binomial system the scientific name has two parts. The first part, written with a capital letter, is the genus. The second part is the species. The coyote is *Canis latrans*, so its genus is *Canis*.

(c) A species is a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring. Both ideas are needed: "can reproduce" alone is not enough, because some different species can mate but their offspring are infertile.
(d) Common names change from country to country and language to language, and one common name can even be used for different animals. The binomial system is internationally agreed, so *Vulpes vulpes* means the same animal to every scientist in the world.

## Q14

**(a)** [1]

**(b)** [3]
- L (1)
- the base sequence of L has the fewest differences from K / L has only 2 bases that differ from K / the DNA of L is the most similar to K (1)
- so L shares the most recent common ancestor with K (1)
- the second and third marks depend on identifying L

**(c)** [2]
- DNA (and its base sequence) is passed on / inherited from ancestors (1)
- organisms that share a more recent common ancestor have more similar base sequences, while organisms that share only a distant ancestor have more differences (1)
- organisms that are not closely related can have similar external features, so base sequences give more reliable evidence of how closely related organisms are (1)
- max 2

**Worked solution**
(a) Compare species K and species M one base at a time, group by group. Group 1 (ATGCC and ATCGC): the 3rd and 4th bases differ, so 2. Group 2 (TAGGA and TTGGA): the 2nd base differs, so 1. Group 3 (TCCTA and GCCAA): the 1st and 4th bases differ, so 2. Group 4 (AGCTT and AACTA): the 2nd and 5th bases differ, so 2. Total = 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 7. Check by counting the matches instead: 13 of the 20 bases match, and 20 - 13 = 7.
(b) The fewer bases that differ, the more similar the DNA. L differs from K at only 2 positions, N at 4 and M at 7. Similar DNA shows that two species have a recent common ancestor, so L is the most closely related to K.
(c) Organisms inherit their DNA from their ancestors. Over time, the base sequences in separate groups change, so groups that split from a common ancestor long ago have more differences, and groups that split recently have fewer. Comparing base sequences therefore shows how closely related organisms are, which is what classification systems aim to show. Appearance can be misleading, because unrelated organisms can look alike.

- any two from: multicellular; cells have no cell walls; no chlorophyll / no chloroplasts / cannot photosynthesise; feed on organic substances made by other living organisms (1) (1)

**(b)** [2]
- it has chlorophyll / chloroplasts, so it photosynthesises / makes its own food (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) The features used to place an organism in the animal kingdom are: it is multicellular; its cells have no cell walls; it has no chlorophyll, so it cannot photosynthesise; and it feeds on organic substances made by other living things.

## Q19

**(a)** [4]
- P: prokaryote (1)
- Q: fungus (1)
- R: protoctist (1)
- S: plant (1)

**(b)** [2]
- R is made of one cell / is unicellular, but plants are multicellular (1)
- R has no cell wall, but plant cells have cell walls made of cellulose (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) P has no nucleus, and its DNA is circular with plasmids, so it is a prokaryote. Q is made of hyphae, has cell walls made of chitin and no chlorophyll, and feeds on dead material, so it is a fungus. R is a single cell with a nucleus. It cannot be a prokaryote (it has a nucleus), an animal (it has chlorophyll), a plant (it is single-celled with no cell wall) or a fungus (no cell wall and it has chlorophyll), so it is a protoctist. S is multicellular, with a nucleus, cellulose cell walls and chlorophyll, so it is a plant.
(b) Having chlorophyll is not enough to make an organism a plant. Plants are multicellular and their cells have cellulose cell walls. R is a single cell and has no cell wall, so it does not have the main features of the plant kingdom. Some protoctists have chlorophyll and photosynthesise, like plants.
