## Q12

**(a)** [2]
- the antibiotic killed the bacteria / the number of live bacteria in flask 1 decreased (1)
- data quote, e.g. from 2000 to 100 in 1 cm³ over 8 hours, or comparison with flask 2 where the number rose to 32 000 (1)
- ALLOW: the number halved every 2 hours between 2 hours and 6 hours
- IGNORE: the antibiotic stopped bacteria growing, if the decrease is also described

**(b)** [2]
- (2000 − 100) ÷ 2000 × 100 or 1900 ÷ 2000 × 100 (1)
- 95 (%) (1)
- ALLOW: 95% with no working for 2 marks

**(c)** [1]
- as a control, to show that the decrease in flask 1 was caused by the antibiotic (1)
- ALLOW: to compare with / to show what happens to the bacteria without antibiotic
- IGNORE: to make it a fair test, on its own

**Worked solution**
(a) In flask 1, with the antibiotic, the number of live bacteria fell from 2000 to 100 in 1 cm³ over 8 hours, so the antibiotic was killing the bacteria. In flask 2, with no antibiotic, the number doubled every 2 hours to 32 000. (b) The decrease is 2000 − 100 = 1900. Percentage decrease = 1900 ÷ 2000 × 100 = 95%. Check: 100 is 5% of 2000, and 100% − 5% = 95%. (c) Flask 2 is a control. It shows that without the antibiotic the bacteria increase, so the fall in flask 1 must be due to the antibiotic.

## Q19

**(a)** [3]
- the antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria (1)
- the resistant bacteria survive and reproduce / multiply (1)
- the gene for resistance is passed on to their offspring (1)
- the population becomes mostly resistant / the proportion of resistant bacteria increases (1)
- max 3
- REJECT: the antibiotic causes the bacteria to become resistant

**(b)** [2]
- bacteria are exposed to antibiotics less often / fewer times (1)
- so there are fewer chances for resistant bacteria to survive while non-resistant bacteria are killed / less selection of resistant bacteria (1)
- ALLOW: resistant bacteria have less of an advantage, so they do not increase in number as quickly
- REJECT: patients become resistant to the antibiotics

**(c)** [1]
- MRSA bacteria are resistant to (several) antibiotics, so the antibiotics are less effective / do not kill them (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) When the antibiotic is used, it kills the bacteria that are not resistant. The few resistant bacteria survive and reproduce, passing on the gene for resistance to their offspring. Each time the antibiotic is used, this happens again, so the resistant bacteria make up more and more of the population. (b) If antibiotics are used only when they are essential, bacteria are exposed to them less often. There are fewer occasions when non-resistant bacteria are killed while resistant ones survive, so resistant strains such as MRSA are selected less often and develop more slowly. (c) MRSA is resistant to several antibiotics, so these antibiotics are less effective against it and fewer treatments work.
