## Q9
- answer: D

**Rationale**
The bacterium first makes a toxin, the toxin causes chloride ions to be secreted into the small intestine, water then follows by osmosis, and the resulting diarrhoea causes dehydration. Option A puts osmosis before the ions are secreted. Option B puts ion secretion before the toxin is made. Option C puts dehydration before the water has moved into the gut.

**Worked solution**

- ALLOW: 99 %; 98 % only if correct working is shown

**(c)** [3]

**Worked solution**

When most people are vaccinated, there are very few infected people to spread the virus, so even unvaccinated people are much less likely to meet it.

**(b)** [3]
Any three from:
- breast milk contains antibodies (1)

**(c)** [2]

**Worked solution**

## Q17

**(a)** [2]
- a bacterium (1)
- transmitted in contaminated water (1)
- REJECT: virus

**(b)** [3]
- the bacterium produces a toxin (1)
- the toxin causes chloride ions to be secreted into the small intestine (1)
- this lowers the water potential in the gut, so water moves into the gut by osmosis (1)
- ALLOW: water moves into the small intestine by osmosis without mention of water potential for the third point

**(c)** [2]
- diarrhoea causes dehydration / loss of a lot of water from the body (1)
- loss of ions from the blood (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Cholera is caused by a bacterium and is spread in water contaminated with it. (b) In the small intestine the bacterium produces a toxin. The toxin makes the cells lining the intestine secrete chloride ions into the gut. This lowers the water potential in the gut, so water moves from the blood and tissues into the gut by osmosis, and this extra water causes diarrhoea. (c) The bacteria stay in the gut, but large amounts of water are lost in the diarrhoea, so the body becomes dehydrated. Ions are also lost from the blood. Both can be life-threatening.

**(a)** [2]
- district K: 60 ÷ 40 000 × 1000 = 1.5 (1)
- district L: 900 ÷ 25 000 × 1000 = 36 (1)

**(b)** [3]
Any three from:
- cholera is caused by a bacterium transmitted in contaminated water (1)
- the faeces of infected people contain the bacterium, so untreated sewage puts it into the river (1)
- people in district L drink river water containing the bacterium (1)
- in district K, water treatment / sewage treatment removes or kills the bacterium, so the drinking water is not contaminated (1)

**(c)** [2]
Any two from:
- not everyone will be vaccinated / the vaccine may not protect everyone (1)
- a clean water supply removes the source of the bacterium / stops transmission of cholera in water (1)
- a clean water supply also prevents the spread of other diseases transmitted in water (1)
- vaccination does not stop the river being contaminated by sewage (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Cases per 1000 people = number of cases ÷ population × 1000. District K: 60 ÷ 40 000 × 1000 = 1.5. District L: 900 ÷ 25 000 × 1000 = 36. Check: 36 × 25 = 900 and 1.5 × 40 = 60. (b) Cholera bacteria leave infected people in their faeces. In district L untreated sewage flows into the river, and people drink that river water, so many swallow the bacterium. District K's water and sewage are treated, which removes or kills the bacteria, so very few people are infected. (c) A vaccination campaign will not reach everyone and may not protect everyone who is vaccinated. While the drinking water is still contaminated, the bacterium keeps reaching people. Clean water removes the source of the infection and also protects against other diseases spread in water.
