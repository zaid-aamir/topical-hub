## Q1

**Rationale**
Asexual reproduction uses one parent and produces genetically identical offspring. Neither gamete fusion nor the mixing of two parents' genes occurs.

## Q2
- answer: D

**Rationale**
Division from one parent without gamete fusion is asexual reproduction. Pollination transfers pollen; fertilisation and sexual reproduction involve gamete nuclei.

## Q3
- answer: A

**Rationale**
Cloning retains the parent's genetic combination. It does not create broad variation or guarantee disease resistance, and it does not require pollination.

## Q4
- answer: C

**Rationale**
Sexual reproduction involves fusion of gamete nuclei. Growth, cell multiplication and production of offspring also occur in asexual reproduction.

## Q5
- answer: D

**Rationale**
Fertilisation is nuclear fusion. Landing on the stigma is pollination, while tube growth enables the nuclei to meet but is not itself fertilisation.

## Q6
- answer: C

**Rationale**
Two haploid sets of 12 combine, giving 12 + 12 = 24 chromosomes. Fertilisation adds sets rather than halving or doubling each set separately.

A spiky (or sticky) surface helps pollen attach to an insect's body. Smooth, small and light grains are characteristic of wind-pollinated flowers and are less likely to be carried by insects.

## Q9
- answer: B

**Rationale**
Pollination transfers pollen from an anther to a stigma. Movement to an ovule occurs later, involving a pollen tube rather than transfer of whole grains.

## Q10

Exposed anthers release pollen into moving air and a feathery stigma traps airborne grains. Nectar, scent and showy petals chiefly attract animals.

## Q12
- answer: A

**Rationale**
Both flowers belong to the same plant, so this is self-pollination. Cross-pollination requires a different plant of the same species.

## Q13
- answer: C

**Rationale**
Cross-pollination transfers pollen to a flower on another plant of the same species. Neither within-plant transfer nor seed movement fits this definition.

## Q14
- answer: A

**Rationale**
Cross-pollination requires transfer between plants, so reproduction can become vulnerable to failure of that transfer. It does not abolish fertilisation or embryo formation.

## Q15

The tube grows from the stigma through the style towards an ovule, carrying the male nucleus. The other routes do not deliver it to the female nucleus.
