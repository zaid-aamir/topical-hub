## Q17

**(a)** [2]
- blood is pumped from the heart to the gills (1)
- (then flows from the gills) to the rest of the body and back to the heart / passes through the heart once in each circuit (1)

**(b)** [3]
- one circuit from the heart to the lungs and back to the heart (1)
- a second circuit from the heart to the rest of the body and back to the heart (1)
- blood passes through the heart twice in each complete circuit (1)
- ALLOW: pulmonary circulation for the lungs circuit and systemic circulation for the body circuit

**(c)** [1]
- left (side) (1)
- ALLOW: left atrium

**Worked solution**
In a fish, the heart pumps blood to the gills, where it picks up oxygen. The blood then carries on to the rest of the body and returns to the heart. It goes through the heart only once on each trip round the body, so this is a single circulation. In a mammal there are two circuits. The right side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs, and the blood returns to the left side of the heart. The left side then pumps this oxygenated blood around the rest of the body, and it returns to the right side. Blood passes through the heart twice on each complete trip, so this is a double circulation.

## Q18

**(a)** [2]
- (4.0 − 2.4) ÷ 4.0 × 100 / 1.6 ÷ 4.0 × 100 (1)
- 40% (1)
- ALLOW: correct answer with no working for 2 marks

**(b)** [4]
- (in a mammal) blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again (1)
- so blood to the body is at a higher pressure / quote 12.0 kPa (in the aorta) compared with 2.4 kPa (to the fish's body) (1)
- so blood flows faster to the tissues / oxygen and glucose are delivered more quickly (1)
- supports a higher rate of respiration / higher metabolic rate / maintaining body temperature (1)
- low pressure to the lungs / quote 2.0 kPa in the pulmonary artery, prevents damage to the lungs (capillaries) / gives more time for gas exchange (1)

- max 4

**Worked solution**
(a) The pressure falls from 4.0 kPa to 2.4 kPa, a decrease of 1.6 kPa. Percentage decrease = 1.6 ÷ 4.0 × 100 = 40%. Check: 40% of 4.0 is 1.6, and 4.0 − 1.6 = 2.4.

(b) In a fish, blood loses a lot of pressure in the narrow vessels of the gills, and it goes straight on to the body at only 2.4 kPa. In a mammal, blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped a second time, so blood leaves in the aorta at 12.0 kPa. This higher pressure moves blood quickly to the tissues, so oxygen and glucose arrive fast enough for a high rate of respiration, which mammals need to stay active and keep their body temperature up. At the same time, the separate circuit to the lungs is at low pressure (2.0 kPa), which protects the delicate lung capillaries and gives time for gas exchange.

**Worked solution**

**Worked solution**
(a) The artery receives blood straight from the heart at high pressure, which surges with each heartbeat. Its thick wall withstands this pressure without bursting.
