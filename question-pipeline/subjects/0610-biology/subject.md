# 0610 Biology – subject rules (draft, from syllabus 2026-2028 Version 3)

All page numbers refer to `reference/0610_syllabus_2026-2028.pdf`.

## 1. Exam data given
- None stated. The syllabus does not provide a data sheet, formula list or equation sheet for any paper (pp. 9, 49-50).
- Calculators may be used in all parts of the exam (p. 54).
- Any numerical data needed for a question is supplied in the question itself; candidates use units consistent with the units supplied and do not convert to other unit systems unless the question asks (p. 57).
- Papers 1 and 2: 40 four-option multiple-choice questions, 45 minutes, 40 marks. Papers 3 and 4: short-answer and structured questions, 1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks (pp. 9, 49).

## 2. Must be recalled, never given
- magnification = image size ÷ actual size (2.2.1); magnification and size calculations in millimetres (2.2.2); converting mm and μm (2.2.3, Supplement).
- Word equation for photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, in the presence of light and chlorophyll (6.1.2).
- Balanced equation for photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (6.1.10, Supplement).
- Word equation for aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (12.2.2).
- Balanced equation for aerobic respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (12.2.3, Supplement).
- Word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast: glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide (12.3.3).
- Balanced equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (12.3.5, Supplement).
- Word equation for anaerobic respiration in muscles: glucose → lactic acid (12.3.4).
- Definitions marked "Define": gene (17.1.2), allele (17.1.3). Every "Describe X as ..." point gives the wording expected for that term (e.g. 1.1.1, 3.1.1, 3.2.7, 3.3.1, 5.1.1).
- Area of a rectangle, triangle and circle; volume of a rectangular block and a cylinder: "recall and use" (p. 55).
- Facts stated in the content, e.g. 23 pairs of chromosomes in a human diploid cell (17.1.12), A–T and C–G base pairing (4.1.4), ABO alleles Iᴬ, Iᴮ and Iᵒ (17.4.15).

## 3. Notation and units
- Chemical formulas with Unicode subscripts as printed in the syllabus: CO₂, H₂O, O₂, C₆H₁₂O₆, C₂H₅OH. Arrows as →.
- Units: mm and μm (2.2); cm³ and dm³ (never l or litre, p. 57); mg, g, kg; °C; s and min. Powers with `^` where Unicode superscripts are unavailable (e.g. cm^3), multiplication with ×, standard form as `3.2 × 10^4`.
- Decimal marker is a dot on the line; 1000–9999 written without commas or spaces; ≥ 10 000 with a space between groups of three digits, e.g. 4 256 789 (p. 57).
- Table headings as `quantity / unit`, e.g. `time / s`; no units in the body of the table (p. 55).
- Naming: binomial system – genus and species, two parts (1.2.3); scientific names are italicised in the syllabus (e.g. *Rhizobium*, 19.3.3). Write genus with a capital letter and species in lower case.
- Use the syllabus's spelling and terms (British spelling): neurone, haemoglobin, oesophagus, faeces, fetus, oestrogen, fertilisation, specialised, hydrogencarbonate indicator, Benedict's solution, biuret test, DCPIP, ethanol emulsion test, partially permeable membrane, water potential, gravitropism, phototropism, vasodilation, vasoconstriction.
- Five kingdoms named: animal, plant, fungus, prokaryote, protoctist (1.3.4). Arthropod groups: myriapods, insects, arachnids, crustaceans; vertebrate groups: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish (1.3.2).
- Genetics ratios written with spaced colons, as printed: 1 : 1 and 3 : 1 (17.4.11).

## 4. Calculation conventions
- Magnification calculations use millimetres as units (2.2.2); Extended candidates may also need mm ↔ μm (2.2.3). Magnification is written with × (e.g. × 400).
- Answers to an appropriate or given number of significant figures or decimal places; round appropriately (p. 54).
- Percentages and percentage change, ratios, mean and range, standard form, simple probability, surface area : volume ratio (pp. 54-55).
- A measurement or calculated quantity must carry a correct unit where appropriate; data recorded to reflect the precision of the instrument and to an appropriate number of significant figures (p. 55).
- Graph readings to half of the smallest square; gradient and intercept with units where appropriate (pp. 55-56).

## 6. Subject-specific checks for the verifier
- Tier: a Core-paper question (Paper 1 or 3 style) must use only `core` points; an Extended question may use Core and Supplement (pp. 9, 49).
- Terminology must match the syllabus wording (e.g. "water potential" for osmosis at Supplement level, 3.2.7; "partially permeable"; "neurone"; "enzyme-substrate complex"; "active site"; "denaturation").
- Check "limited to" scopes: do not test content outside the listed items (e.g. vitamins limited to C and D, 7.1.2; mineral ions limited to calcium and iron, 7.1.2; specialised cells limited to the six listed, 2.1.6).
- Respect every "not required" note: e.g. full names of bases (4.1.4), details of lignification (8.1.3), details of clotting (9.4.3), stages of sewage treatment (10.1.5), details of nephron processes (13.1.5), endosperm and development (16.3.12), nucleotide structure (17.1.5), transcription or translation details (17.1.8), stages of mitosis and meiosis (17.2.1, 17.3.2), generic names of bacteria such as Rhizobium (19.3.3), genetic drift (20.4.9), specific details of inserting plasmids (21.3.3).
- Equations must be exactly the syllabus forms in section 2; anaerobic respiration in yeast gives "alcohol" in the word equation and C₂H₅OH in the balanced equation.
- Genetics: phenotypic ratios at Core are limited to 1 : 1 and 3 : 1 (17.4.11); codominance and sex linkage are Supplement (17.4.14-17.4.18).
- Command words must be used with the meanings on p. 58; MCQ items have exactly four options (p. 49).
- Numbers and units follow p. 57 (dm³ not litre; spaced thousands ≥ 10 000).

## 7. Anything else
- Questions may require candidates to apply material to unfamiliar contexts and to apply knowledge from one area of the syllabus to another (AO1, p. 10). AO2 questions may use unfamiliar information requiring logical, deductive application (p. 10).
- AO weightings: Papers 1-4 are 63% AO1 and 37% AO2 (p. 11).
- Practical-skills content (pp. 50-56) is assessed in Papers 5 and 6 (AO3) and is not part of the numbered subject content; points beginning "Investigate" in the subject content are numbered learning points and are mapped.
- Hazard codes are for familiarity only and are not assessed (p. 52).
