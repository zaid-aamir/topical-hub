# 4BI1 Biology – subject rules (draft, from Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3)

Specification: Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Biology (4BI1), Issue 3 (footer "Specification – Issue 3 – September 2024"), first teaching September 2017, first examination June 2019 (PDF p. 1, p. 7). All page numbers below are PDF page numbers of `reference/4BI1_specification.pdf`; the printed page number on content pages is the PDF page minus 6 (subject content = PDF pp. 17-32, printed pp. 11-26).

**Paper rule.** The qualification is untiered: every student sits Paper 1 (4BI1/1B, 2 hours, 110 marks, 61.1%) and Paper 2 (4BI1/2B, 1 hour 15 minutes, 70 marks, 38.9%) in the same series (PDF pp. 7, 13-14, 33). Paper 1 assesses only content that is not in bold and has no 'B' reference; Paper 2 assesses all the content, including the bold 'B' statements ("Bold statements cover some sub-topics in greater depth"); on both papers questions may come from any topic (PDF pp. 13-14, 33). In the pipeline, tier `core` = statement number without a B suffix (Paper 1 & 2); tier `supplement` = statement number with a B suffix, e.g. 2.40B (Paper 2 only). Never call these Core/Extended, and never call the qualification "Cambridge" or "IGCSE 0610". 176 statements: 134 Paper 1 & 2, 42 Paper 2 only.

## 1. Exam data given
- None stated. The specification lists no data sheet, formula sheet, equation list or periodic table for either paper (PDF pp. 7-8, 13-14, 33-35, 43-55).
- A calculator may be used in both papers; students are expected to have a suitable electronic calculator for all papers; calculators that can retrieve text or formulae, or with QWERTY keyboards, are not allowed (PDF pp. 13-14, 34).
- Because nothing is provided, every number, unfamiliar organism, context fact, indicator colour key or formula that a question needs (other than the recall items in section 2) must be given in the question itself. This is an inference from the absence of a data sheet, not a stated rule.

## 2. Must be recalled, never given
The specification states the equations must be known but does not print them. Use these standard forms:
- Photosynthesis, word and balanced chemical symbol equation, "know" (2.19; process 2.18): carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light energy absorbed by chlorophyll); 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
- Aerobic respiration in living organisms, word and balanced chemical symbol equation, "know" (2.37): glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water; C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. Respiration produces ATP and ATP provides energy for cells (2.34, 2.35).
- Anaerobic respiration, word equation only, "know" (2.38): in animals glucose → lactic acid; in plants glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide. The same plant/yeast equation underlies 5.5 and 5.6 (yeast in bread making, investigating anaerobic respiration by yeast). No balanced symbol equation for anaerobic respiration is required, so never demand one.
- No magnification formula and no other quantitative biology formula appears in the content. If a calculation needs any formula (magnification, percentage change, rate, mean, surface area : volume ratio), it is not on a recall list in the specification: give it in the question, or make sure only Appendix 4 skills are needed.
- Numerical facts in the content: diploid number 46 and haploid number 23 in human cells (3.32); XX female, XY male (3.26); about 10% of energy transferred between trophic levels (4.9); mitosis gives two genetically identical cells, meiosis four haploid, genetically different cells (3.28, 3.30).
- Base pairing A–T and C–G, two strands in a double helix; RNA single stranded with uracil (U) instead of thymine (T) (3.16B, 3.17B; Paper 2 only). Full base names are printed in the specification (adenine, thymine, cytosine, guanine, uracil) and may be required.
- Named lists the student must know: characteristics of living organisms (1.1); features of plants, animals, fungi, protoctists, bacteria and viruses including the named examples (1.2-1.4); cell structures and functions (2.2, 2.3); elements in carbohydrates, proteins and lipids (2.7); diet components including vitamins A, C and D and the mineral ions calcium and iron (2.25); digestive enzymes and products (2.29); thorax structures (2.46); blood components (2.59); plasma transport list (2.60); nephron regions (2.74B); hormones adrenaline, insulin, testosterone, progesterone, oestrogen (2.94) and ADH, FSH, LH (2.95B); greenhouse gases water vapour, carbon dioxide, nitrous oxide, methane and CFCs (4.13); fermenter conditions (5.8); fish farming methods (5.9B).
- Term meanings: genome and gene (3.14); allele (3.19); dominant, recessive, homozygous, heterozygous, phenotype, genotype (3.20); codominance (3.21B); polygenic inheritance (3.22); mutation (3.34); homeostasis (2.81); transpiration (2.56B); population, community, habitat, ecosystem (4.1); biodiversity (4.3B); trophic level names (4.6); transgenic (5.16); micropropagation (tissue culture), explants, in vitro (5.17B). Where the specification gives the meaning (2.81, 2.56B, 3.14, 3.34, 5.16), mark schemes should use that wording.
- Appendix 4 mathematical skills ticked for Biology are "developed and assessed" (PDF pp. 49-50), including the area of triangles and rectangles and the surface area and volume of cubes (5C), which students work out without a given formula.

## 3. Notation and units
- Chemical formulas with Unicode subscripts: CO₂, H₂O, O₂, C₆H₁₂O₆, SO₂ (sulfur dioxide, 4.12), CO (carbon monoxide). Arrows as →.
- The specification states no unit or number-format rules. Use SI and conventional biology units in plain text: mm, μm, cm, m; cm³ and dm³ (write powers as cm^3 only if Unicode superscripts are unavailable); g, kg, mg; °C; s, min; kJ or J for energy (2.33B). Use × for multiplication and standard form as `3.2 × 10^4` (standard form is Appendix 4 skill 1B). Table column headings as `quantity / unit`. These are house conventions, not specification rules.
- Naming: the specification prints genus and species names in italics (Mucor, Amoeba, Chlorella, Plasmodium in 1.2; Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Pneumococcus in 1.3; Lactobacillus in 5.7) and italicises "in vitro" (5.17B). In Markdown write them in italics (*Lactobacillus bulgaricus*), genus capitalised, species lower case. The specification does not state a binomial-naming rule or require classification beyond the five groups in 1.2-1.4 (plants, animals, fungi, protoctists, bacteria) plus viruses.
- Specification spelling and terms (British): organisation, specialised, fertilisation, haemoglobin, oesophagus, oestrogen, co-ordination, co-ordinated, micro-organisms, sulfur (not sulphur), fertiliser, yoghurt, polythene tunnels, glasshouses, dietary fibre, lipid (fats and oils), hydrogen-carbonate indicator (2.45B, hyphenated), geotropic and phototropic (2.84; not gravitropic), auxin, neurotransmitters, electrical impulses, vasoconstriction and vasodilation, Bowman’s capsule, glomerulus, glomerular filtrate, ultrafiltration, selective reabsorption, proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, collecting duct, osmoregulation, ADH, FSH, LH, amniotic fluid, pollen tube, runners, cuttings, double helix, mRNA, tRNA, codons, anticodons, codominance, polygenic inheritance, monohybrid, family pedigrees, nitrogen fixing bacteria, nitrifying bacteria, denitrifying bacteria, pyramids of number, pyramids of biomass, pyramids of energy transfer, enhanced greenhouse effect, eutrophication, evapotranspiration, aseptic precautions, oxygenation and agitation, intraspecific and interspecific predation, biological control, restriction enzymes, ligase, vectors, recombinant DNA, transgenic, micropropagation (tissue culture), explants, enucleated egg cell, Dolly the sheep.
- Genetics: write genetic diagrams and Punnett squares as Markdown tables; use a capital letter for the dominant allele and the same lower-case letter for the recessive allele (house convention; not stated in the specification). Sex chromosomes as XX and XY (3.26). Ratios with spaced colons, e.g. 3 : 1.

## 4. Calculation conventions
- The specification states no significant-figure or rounding rule beyond Appendix 4 skill 2A "Use an appropriate number of significant figures" (PDF p. 49). Give answers to a sensible number of significant figures (usually matching the data) and always with a unit where the quantity has one.
- Calculation types ticked for Biology in Appendix 4 (PDF pp. 49-50): decimal and standard form; ratios, fractions, percentages, powers and roots; estimates without a calculator; arithmetic mean; mode and median; simple probability (3.25 monohybrid outcomes); sampling principles (4.2, 4.4B quadrats); bar charts, frequency tables, histograms and scatter diagrams; order of magnitude; change the subject of an equation, substitute values with units and solve simple equations; translate between graphs and numbers; plot two variables; slope and intercept of a linear graph; areas of triangles and rectangles, surface areas and volumes of cubes (2.16 surface area to volume ratio).
- Not ticked for Biology, so do not require: sin x, the symbols <, >, ∝, ~ (3A), y = mx + c (4B), tangent to a curve as rate of change (4E), area under a curve (4F), angles (5A), 2D/3D representation (5B).
- Typical biology calculations within these skills: percentage change in mass (osmosis, 2.17), rate = change ÷ time, mean of repeats, estimated population from quadrat counts (4.2), energy transfer percentage (4.9), energy content of food (2.33B; give any formula used, e.g. energy = mass of water × 4.2 × temperature rise, since it is not in the specification), probabilities and ratios from crosses (3.25).

## 6. Subject-specific checks for the verifier
- Paper rule: a `core` question may use only statements without the B suffix; a `supplement` question must use at least one B statement. Watch for B content slipping into core items, e.g. plant gas exchange and stomata (2.40B-2.45B), root hair water uptake and transpiration (2.55B-2.58B), vaccination and memory cells (2.63B), clotting (2.64B), all kidney/nephron/ADH content (2.72B-2.79B; core only has 2.71 excretory products), ADH/FSH/LH (2.95B, 3.10B), DNA/RNA structure and protein synthesis (3.16B-3.18B), codominance (3.21B), effects and causes of mutation (3.35B-3.37B), biodiversity (4.3B, 4.4B), nitrogen cycle (4.11B), deforestation (4.18B), fish farming (5.9B), micropropagation and cloning (5.17B-5.20B), pH practical (2.14B; but pH and the active site is core 2.13), energy content of food practical (2.33B), cell differentiation and stem cells (2.5B, 2.6B).
- Terminology must match the specification wording (section 3), e.g. "geotropic" not "gravitropic", "hydrogen-carbonate indicator", "active site", "biological catalysts", "pyramids of energy transfer", "Bowman’s capsule", "ultrafiltration", "selective reabsorption".
- Respect "including" lists as the required detail (PDF p. 7) and do not go beyond them: e.g. vitamins limited to A, C and D and mineral ions to calcium and iron (2.25); enzymes amylase, maltase, proteases, lipases (2.29); factors for photosynthesis CO₂ concentration, light intensity, temperature (2.20); transpiration factors humidity, wind speed, temperature, light intensity (2.57B); plasma transports carbon dioxide, digested food, urea, hormones and heat energy (2.60); red blood cell adaptations shape, no nucleus, haemoglobin (2.61); hormones only those named in 2.94, 2.95B; air pollutants sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide (4.12); asexual reproduction illustrated by runners and cuttings (3.7); cloned mammals illustrated by Dolly the sheep (5.19B).
- Respect stated exclusions: specific names of bacteria in the nitrogen cycle are not required (4.11B); only word equations for anaerobic respiration (2.38); balanced equations only for photosynthesis and aerobic respiration (2.19, 2.37).
- Examples in the content are illustrative only (PDF p. 7): do not require a student to recall a particular example organism (e.g. maize, housefly, Chlorella) unless the question supplies it; examples named with "illustrated by" are required.
- Equations in mark schemes must be the forms in section 2. For anaerobic respiration in plants expect glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide; whether to ALLOW "alcohol" for ethanol is a mark-scheme choice the specification does not settle (house choice: ALLOW).
- Mathematical demand must stay within the Biology-ticked Appendix 4 skills (section 4).
- Command words must be used with the meanings in Appendix 5 (PDF pp. 51-52). "Describe" answers are developed statements without required reasons; "Explain" needs reasoning; "Determine" needs a quantitative element; "Suggest" is for novel contexts; "Give/State/Name" are recall. MCQs use the direct command words What, Why, Which.
- Practical statements (14, all starting "practical:"): questions test method, apparatus (described in words), independent/dependent/control variables, safety, results tables, calculations, conclusions, reliability, accuracy and validity, and improvements (PDF p. 34). Check that the practical context matches the specification statement (e.g. 2.45B uses hydrogen-carbonate indicator; 4.2 and 4.4B use quadrats; 2.39 respiring seeds or other suitable organisms; 5.6 yeast in different conditions). Suggested practicals in Appendix 6 (PDF p. 53) are not specification statements; they may be used as contexts only if every fact needed is given or is in a mapped statement.

## 7. Anything else
- Both papers use "a mixture of different question styles, including multiple-choice questions, short-answer questions, calculations and extended open-response questions" (PDF pp. 13-14, 33). Multiple-choice items exist but the papers are mostly structured; the specification does not state the number of options per MCQ or any mark tariff (no "6-mark" question is mentioned). Use four options A-D (house convention) and keep extended open-response parts as longer "Describe/Explain/Discuss/Evaluate" parts.
- Calculators allowed in both papers (PDF pp. 13-14, 34).
- Assessment objectives (PDF p. 35): AO1 knowledge and understanding 38-42%; AO2 application, analysis and evaluation 38-42%; AO3 experimental skills, analysis and evaluation of data and methods 19-21%. Paper 1: AO1 23.2-25.7%, AO2 23.2-25.7%, AO3 11.6-12.8%; Paper 2: AO1 14.8-16.3%, AO2 14.8-16.3%, AO3 7.4-8.2%. About a fifth of marks are practical-skills (AO3) marks in the written papers, so batches should include data-handling and method questions.
- Aims (PDF p. 9): use unifying patterns and themes in new and changing situations; apply principles to different contexts; evaluate information; analyse, interpret and evaluate data and methods and suggest improvements. Applied questions in unfamiliar contexts are expected, with the unfamiliar information given.
- Some statements carry unnumbered group headings in the specification ("Flowering plants", "Humans", "Crop plants", "Micro-organisms", "Fish farming"). The map records these as a `group` field on the point; use them to set the context (e.g. 2.40B "the role of diffusion in gas exchange" is about flowering plants, not humans; 2.70 is excretion in plants).
- Assessment is in English only (PDF p. 37).
- These are original exam-style topical questions, not Pearson past papers. Do not copy or lightly reword any Pearson Edexcel or Cambridge exam question.
