## Q15
**(a)** Urea in the plasma reaches the kidney in the renal artery and passes into the glomerulus (capillary ball). By ultrafiltration, high pressure forces small molecules including urea and water out of the glomerulus through the filter into the Bowman's capsule. The filtrate flows into the proximal convoluted tubule, where selective reabsorption returns all glucose (and some water and ions) to the blood by active transport; urea stays in the tubule. It flows through the loop of Henle and the distal convoluted tubule to the collecting duct, where water is reabsorbed by osmosis (controlled by ADH), concentrating the urea in the urine. Urine then passes down the ureter to the bladder, where it is stored, and leaves the body through the urethra.
**(b)** The glomerulus (the filter, i.e. glomerulus / Bowman's capsule filtration barrier) is damaged, so it lets large protein molecules through into the filtrate; they are not reabsorbed and so appear in urine.
