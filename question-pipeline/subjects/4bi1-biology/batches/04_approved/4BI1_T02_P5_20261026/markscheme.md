## Q1
- answer: C

**Rationale**
Urea is a waste product of metabolism, and some of it is excreted in sweat. Glucose is a useful nutrient that the body keeps, so it is not an excretory product. Starch is a plant storage carbohydrate and is not in human blood or sweat. Protein molecules are useful and too large to be lost in sweat.

**Worked solution**
The skin is one of the three organs of excretion in humans, along with the lungs and kidneys. Sweat contains water, ions and a small amount of urea. Of the options, only urea is a waste product of metabolism.

## Q2
- answer: B

**Rationale**
Oxygen is made by photosynthesis, and the oxygen the leaf does not use leaves through the stomata as a waste product. Carbon dioxide is a waste product of respiration and a raw material for photosynthesis, so it is not made by photosynthesis. Glucose is made by photosynthesis but is used by the plant, not lost as waste. Water vapour is lost from leaves, but water is a raw material of photosynthesis, not a product.

**Worked solution**
Recall the photosynthesis equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen. The two products are glucose and oxygen. The plant uses the glucose, so the waste product is oxygen.

## Q4
- answer: A

**Rationale**
The collecting duct is the last region of the nephron: fluid in it becomes urine and drains towards the ureter. The Bowman’s capsule is where filtrate first enters the nephron. The proximal convoluted tubule comes next, then the loop of Henle, so neither of these is last.

**Worked solution**
The order through a nephron is Bowman’s capsule → proximal convoluted tubule → loop of Henle → second convoluted tubule → collecting duct. So the collecting duct comes last.

## Q5
- answer: D

**Rationale**
Urine from a healthy person contains water, urea and ions. Glucose is not normally present because it is all reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule. Red blood cells and protein are too large to be filtered out of the blood in the glomerulus, so they are not normally in urine.

**Worked solution**
Ultrafiltration lets only small molecules into the nephron. Useful glucose is then reabsorbed. The fluid that remains, urine, contains water, urea and ions.

## Q15

**(a)** [6]
- urea enters the glomerulus in the blood (1)
- blood in the glomerulus is at high pressure OR ultrafiltration occurs (1)
- urea is a small molecule, so it is forced out of the capillaries into the Bowman’s capsule (1)
- urea becomes part of the glomerular filtrate (1)
- the filtrate passes through the proximal convoluted tubule, the loop of Henle and the second convoluted tubule (any two regions named in the correct order) (1)
- ALLOW distal convoluted tubule for second convoluted tubule
- much of the urea is not reabsorbed (stays in the filtrate), while glucose is reabsorbed (1)
- urea enters the collecting duct, where water is reabsorbed, so the urea becomes more concentrated (1)
- urine passes down the ureter (1)
- urine is stored in the bladder (1)
- urine leaves the body through the urethra (1)
- max 6

**(b)** [2]
- glomerulus OR Bowman’s capsule (the filter) (1)
- protein molecules are normally too large to be filtered, so a damaged filter lets protein through into the filtrate (and urine) (1)
- ALLOW: protein is not normally reabsorbed, so it must have entered the nephron through the filter (second mark)

**Worked solution**
(a) Blood carrying urea enters the kidney and flows into a glomerulus. The blood there is at high pressure, so small molecules, including urea, are forced out of the capillaries into the Bowman’s capsule. This is ultrafiltration, and the liquid formed is the glomerular filtrate. The filtrate flows through the proximal convoluted tubule, where glucose is reabsorbed but much of the urea stays in the filtrate. It then passes around the loop of Henle and along the second convoluted tubule into the collecting duct. In the collecting duct water is reabsorbed into the blood, so the urea becomes more concentrated. The fluid, now urine, flows down the ureter to the bladder, is stored there, and finally leaves the body through the urethra.
(b) Protein molecules are normally too big to pass from the glomerulus into the Bowman’s capsule, so healthy urine has no protein. If protein appears in urine, the filter formed by the glomerulus and Bowman’s capsule is probably damaged and is letting large molecules through.
