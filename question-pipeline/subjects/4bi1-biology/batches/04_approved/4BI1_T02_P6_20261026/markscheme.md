## Q1
- answer: B

**Rationale**
A co-ordinated response starts with a change (the stimulus), which is detected by a receptor; the information is passed on and an effector carries out the response. A puts the receptor before the stimulus, C has the effector acting before anything has detected the change, and D starts with the effector.

**Worked solution**
Think about what has to happen first: nothing can respond until something has changed (stimulus) and that change has been detected (receptor). Only then can an effector, such as a muscle or gland, bring about the response.

## Q3
- answer: A

**Rationale**
An impulse arrives at the end of the first neurone (2), which causes neurotransmitter to be released (4); the neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap (3) and binds to receptors on the next neurone (1). B has release before the impulse arrives, C has diffusion before release, and D has binding before the neurotransmitter has crossed the gap.

**Worked solution**
Follow the signal in time: electrical impulse arrives → chemical released → chemical crosses gap → chemical binds to the next neurone, which starts a new impulse there.

## Q12

**(a)** [4]
- receptors (in the skin) detect the heat (stimulus) (1)
- an (electrical) impulse passes along a sensory neurone to the spinal cord (1)
- the impulse passes to a relay neurone in the spinal cord and then to a motor neurone (1)
- the motor neurone carries the impulse to a muscle (effector), which contracts and moves the finger away (1)
- ALLOW: impulses cross synapses between the neurones
- ALLOW: CNS for spinal cord

**(b)** [2]
- the response is rapid OR automatic (does not need conscious thought) (1)
- so less damage is done to the skin OR the burn is less serious (1)

**Worked solution**
The heat is the stimulus. Receptors in the skin of the finger detect it and send an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone to the spinal cord. There the impulse passes, across synapses, through a relay neurone to a motor neurone. The motor neurone carries the impulse to a muscle in the arm, the effector, which contracts and pulls the finger away. Because the impulse does not have to wait for a conscious decision, the response is very fast, so the finger is in contact with the hot pan for less time and less damage is done.

**(b)** [2]

**Worked solution**
