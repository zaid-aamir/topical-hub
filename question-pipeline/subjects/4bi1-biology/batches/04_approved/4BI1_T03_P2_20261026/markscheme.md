## Q3

In DNA, adenine (A) pairs with thymine (T) and cytosine (C) pairs with guanine (G), so ATGCCA pairs with TACGGT. B uses uracil (U), which is found in RNA, not DNA. C simply repeats the original strand. D pairs the bases wrongly (A with G and T with C).

## Q13

**(a)** [3]

**(b)** [1]

**(c)** [1]

**Worked solution**
(a) In the nucleus, the DNA of the gene unwinds and the two strands separate. One strand is used as a template, and an mRNA molecule is made with bases complementary to it. The mRNA then leaves the nucleus and travels to a ribosome.
(b) mRNA is complementary to the template strand, with uracil (U) pairing with adenine (A) instead of thymine: T→A, A→U, C→G, G→C. So TAC → AUG, GGA → CCU and TTT → AAA. The mRNA is AUG CCU AAA. Check: pairing back from AUG gives TAC, which matches.
(c) Look up each mRNA codon in the table: AUG = methionine, CCU = proline, AAA = lysine. (Reading the template codons TAC, GGA and TTT directly is a common mistake: the table entries UAC, GGA and UUU would give the wrong amino acids.)
(d) During translation, each tRNA molecule carries one specific amino acid to the ribosome. The tRNA has three bases called an anticodon, which pair with the complementary codon on the mRNA. This makes sure each amino acid is placed in the right position, so the amino acids are joined in the order coded for by the mRNA.
