## Q2
- answer: D

**Rationale**
Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides, so zinc + oxygen → zinc oxide is correct. A is wrong because calcium and cold water form calcium hydroxide (not the oxide) and hydrogen. B is wrong because copper does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid. C is wrong because magnesium and steam form magnesium oxide (not the hydroxide) and hydrogen.

**Worked solution**
Check each equation against the general patterns:
- metal + oxygen → metal oxide (D fits this pattern and is correct)
- reactive metal + cold water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen (so A should give calcium hydroxide)
- metal + steam → metal oxide + hydrogen (so C should give magnesium oxide)

A common mistake is to mix up the water and steam products: cold water gives the hydroxide, steam gives the oxide.

## Q13
- answer: A

**Rationale**
A metal displaces another metal from a solution of its ions only if it is more reactive. Zinc is more reactive than silver (1 reacts), and magnesium is more reactive than zinc (4 reacts). Copper is less reactive than iron (2 does not react), and iron is less reactive than magnesium (3 does not react). **Worked solution**
Use the order Mg > Zn > Fe > Cu > Ag. For each experiment, ask whether the solid metal is higher in the series than the metal whose ions are in solution.

- 2: Cu vs Fe. Cu is lower, so no reaction.

**(a)** [1]
- coating iron / steel with (a layer of) zinc (1)

**(b)** [1]
- the zinc layer stops / excludes oxygen (air) and water from reaching the steel (1)

**(c)** [3]
- zinc is more reactive than iron / zinc is above iron in the reactivity series (1)
- zinc loses electrons (to form Zn2+ ions) more readily than iron / zinc is oxidised instead of iron (1)
- so iron does not lose electrons / does not form ions, and the zinc corrodes instead (sacrificial protection) (1)
- ALLOW Zn → Zn2+ + 2e− as evidence for the second mark

**(d)** [2]
- copper is less reactive than iron / below iron in the reactivity series (1)

**Worked solution**

(a) Choose a method that suits the object. A garden gate is large, stays outdoors and is not a moving machine part, so paint works well and lasts for years. A dish rack is wet all the time and touches plates, so a waterproof plastic coating is ideal, and it does not chip easily or contaminate food. Grease suits moving parts such as chains and hinges, not objects that people touch or that hold food.
(b) Iron rusts only when both oxygen and water reach it. A barrier method covers the surface with a layer (paint, plastic or grease) that keeps oxygen and water away from the metal, so rusting cannot start.
(c) The scratch breaks the barrier. The iron under the scratch now meets air (oxygen) and rain (water), so it rusts there. Everywhere else, the unbroken paint still keeps oxygen and water out, so no rust forms.

## Q29

**(a)** [2]
- Al3+ + 3e− → Al (2)
- correct species but wrong number of electrons (1)

**(b)** [2]
- 2O2− → O2 + 4e− (2)
- ALLOW 2O2− − 4e− → O2 (2)

**(c)** [2]
- (the oxygen formed at the anode) reacts with the carbon (at the high temperature) (1)
- to form carbon dioxide, so the anodes burn away / get smaller (1)

**Worked solution**
(a) Aluminium ions are positive (Al3+) and are attracted to the negative cathode. Each ion gains three electrons to become an aluminium atom: Al3+ + 3e− → Al. Check the charge: 3+ plus 3− gives 0 on each side.
(b) Oxide ions (O2−) are attracted to the positive anode. They lose electrons, and pairs of oxygen atoms join to form O2 molecules: 2O2− → O2 + 4e−. Check: 2 O on each side; charge 4− on the left and 0 + 4(−) = 4− on the right.
(c) The cell runs at a high temperature, and the oxygen made at the anode reacts with the hot carbon to form carbon dioxide: carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide. The carbon anodes are slowly used up (they burn away), so they must be replaced regularly.

## Q30

**(a)** [1]
- gold is very unreactive, so it does not react with (oxygen or water or) other substances to form compounds (1)
- ALLOW gold is at the bottom of the reactivity series

**(b)** [1]
- carbon is more reactive than zinc and iron (so it can remove the oxygen from their oxides) (1)
- ALLOW zinc and iron are below carbon in the reactivity series

**(c)** [1]
- sodium is more reactive than carbon / sodium is above carbon in the reactivity series (1)

**(d)** [1]
- the higher the metal is in the reactivity series (the more reactive it is), the harder it is to obtain from its ore (1)
- ALLOW the reverse: the lower / less reactive the metal, the easier it is to obtain

**Worked solution**
(a) Gold is at the bottom of the reactivity series. It is so unreactive that it has not combined with oxygen or other elements over time, so it is found as the pure metal.
(b) Carbon is above zinc and iron in the reactivity series. When their oxides are heated with carbon, the more reactive carbon removes the oxygen (reduces the oxide) and leaves the metal.
(c) Sodium is above carbon, so carbon cannot take the oxygen (or other element) away from sodium. Instead, a strong method, electrolysis, is needed.
(d) The general rule is that the more reactive a metal is, the more tightly it holds on to other elements in its compounds, and the harder (and more expensive) it is to extract. Unreactive metals such as gold are the easiest to obtain. Very reactive metals such as sodium and aluminium need electrolysis.
