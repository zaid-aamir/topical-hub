## Q25
**(a)** In pure aluminium, atoms of the same size are arranged in regular layers/rows. Because all the atoms are the same size, the layers can slide over each other easily when a force is applied, so pure aluminium is soft.
**(b)** Magnesium atoms are a different size from aluminium atoms, so they disrupt the regular layers of aluminium atoms. This makes it harder for the layers to slide over each other, so more force is needed to deform the alloy — it is harder than pure aluminium.
**(c)** The student is wrong because an alloy is a mixture, not a compound: the elements in it are not chemically combined together in a fixed ratio/proportion. In a compound the atoms are chemically bonded in a fixed ratio; in an alloy the atoms are simply mixed and the proportions can vary.
