## Q9

**(a)** [1]

## Q10

**(b)** [1]
- C6H12 (1)

- CH2 (1)

**Worked solution**
6 carbon atoms and 12 hydrogen atoms in one molecule give the molecular formula C6H12. The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of these atoms: dividing both numbers by their highest common factor, 6, gives a ratio of 1:2, so the empirical formula is CH2. ## Q11

**(c)** [3]

**Worked solution**

## Q12

**(a)** [2]
- HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l) (2)
- correct formulae and balancing but a missing or incorrect state symbol (1)

**(b)** [1]
- Na+ and Cl− (1)
- ALLOW: either order; must name both to gain the mark

**(c)** [2]
- H+(aq) + OH−(aq) → H2O(l) (2)
- correct species (H+ and OH− forming water) but a state symbol missing or wrong (1)

**Worked solution**
The full equation is HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l); it is already balanced, with 1 of each atom type on both sides. Sodium ions, Na+, and chloride ions, Cl−, appear unchanged on both sides of the equation (they start dissolved and end up dissolved, in the same form), so they are the spectator ions. Removing them from the full ionic version of the equation leaves only the ions that actually react: H+(aq) + OH−(aq) → H2O(l). This shows the essential chemistry of any strong acid–strong alkali neutralisation: hydrogen ions combine with hydroxide ions to form water.

## Q30

**(a)** [3]
- moles per 100 g: C = 85.7 ÷ 12 = 7.14; H = 14.3 ÷ 1 = 14.3 (1)
- ratio C : H = 7.14 : 14.3, dividing by the smaller value gives 1 : 2.00 (1)
- empirical formula CH2 (1)

**(b)** [2]
- mass of empirical formula CH2 = 12 + 2 = 14; 42 ÷ 14 = 3, so molecular formula = C3H6 (1)
- final answer C3H6 (1)

**Worked solution**
To find the empirical formula, convert each percentage to an amount in moles using the Ar values, treating the percentages as masses in 100 g of compound: moles of C = 85.7 ÷ 12 = 7.14; moles of H = 14.3 ÷ 1 = 14.3. Dividing both by the smaller number, 7.14, gives a ratio of C : H = 1 : 2.00, so the empirical formula is CH2. The mass of this empirical formula unit is 12 + (2 × 1) = 14. Since the relative molecular mass of the actual compound is 42, the molecular formula must be a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula: 42 ÷ 14 = 3. Multiplying every number in CH2 by 3 gives the molecular formula, C3H6.
